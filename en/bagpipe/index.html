The bagpipe

A bag stores the breath, a chanter carries the melody and drones hold the bass, so the sound never stops for a breath

1 min read71 characters条目World InstrumentsUpdated 2026-10-28

A bagpipe's mechanism is not in the pipes but in the bag. A mouth or bellows only fills it; the bag feeds a chanter and drones at steady pressure.

The main line and structure: bag, chanter, drones

It is a chain of energy: source → bag → reed → pipe. The chanter has finger holes and carries the melody; the drones have none and hold one note each. The bag smooths the pulses of the lungs, which separates it from a shawm: a shawm's phrases must leave room for breath, a bagpipe's need not.

PartJobKey point
Bagstores air, smooths pressureleather or synthetic; pressed by the arm
Blowpiperefills the bagone-way valve, air in but not out
Chanterplays the melodydouble reed, conical bore, finger holes; nine notes on the Highland pipe
Droneshold the basssingle reeds, cylindrical bores; tonic, fifth or octave

The price of continuity: breath and dynamics

While the player inhales, the arm keeps pressing, so no breath is heard. The price is dynamics: pressure must stay steady or the drones drift; accents come from gracenotes and fingering, not from air.

The Irish uilleann pipes are the counterexample: bellows supply air, the chanter can be stopped on the knee, and three regulators add chords. "A bagpipe cannot stop" is true only of the mouth-blown pipe.

Range and timbre

The Highland nine are G, A, B, C♯, D, E, F♯, G, A, with no natural C, hence its modal colour. The drones tune to A: two tenors on A3, one bass on A2.

The family, and the hurdy-gurdy

Against the hurdy-gurdy: both build in a drone, but a bagpipe moves an air column, a hurdy-gurdy a string.

Common misconceptions

  • "The bagpipe was invented in Scotland." Bagpipes are spread across Europe, North Africa and West Asia; the Highland pipe is only the most famous branch.
  • "The player blows the sound directly." The mouth only refills the bag; its pressure drives the reeds.
  • "A bagpipe can crescendo." The Highland pipe hardly can; steady pressure keeps it in tune.

Three things to take away

1. The bag is the mechanism: it turns intermittent breath into continuous pressure. 2. Chanter plus drones: melody and bass come from the same bag of air. 3. Continuity has a price: no breath seam and almost no dynamics; the uilleann pipe buys another possibility with bellows and stopping.
