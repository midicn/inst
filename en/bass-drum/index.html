Bass Drum

The two-headed membranophone with the deepest low end — in orchestra and drum kit alike

2 min readPercussionUpdated 2026-09-26

instrumentpercussionwestern

One name, two instruments

A bass drum stroke in an orchestra lowers the whole band by an inch. It does one job: it supplies weight at low frequency. Same class as the snare, a membranophone with no definite pitch, but all its energy sits at the bottom. The trouble is the name, which points at two different objects — the drum hung vertically in a hall, and the horizontal kick of a kit worked by a pedal.

Two heads, no snares

No snares is the decisive part. A snare gets its high frequencies from the wire set under the head; the bass drum has none, so the energy lands cleanly low. Heads often exceed 80 cm across, and a larger head lowers the fundamental while making the ring harder to control. Orchestras hang it vertically and strike from the side with one soft mallet, and the sound spreads in all directions. Hung that way the head stands at right angles to the floor and the low end travels outward; laid flat, the head faces the audience and the shell pushes the sound forward.

One principle, two jobs

The kit lays the same drum on its side and drives a beater with a pedal: short, directional, marking the placement of every beat. Acoustically identical, musically two instruments, one wanting resonance and the other control. That is why an orchestral player says bass drum and a kit player says kick, and they are not talking about the same thing.

The stroke makes the instrument

Unpitched, so no range. What varies is stroke and force: a light single stroke is distant thunder, a heavy one is proclamation, alternating mallets roll a sustained roar, both together is the heaviest accent, and a hand on the head cuts it short. In an orchestra it is a scoring device rather than a rhythm instrument; it appears rarely and each time changes the weight of the sound. Nineteenth-century Turkish fashion brought it into European bands, Mozart and Beethoven used it, and it still works beside the timpani: the timpani supply the note, the bass drum the mass. Double pedals turn that placement into a continuous push, which is what rock and funk kicks are built on.
