The balafon

Its name is an action — the whole craft of playing this wooden keyboard

1 min read467 characters条目World InstrumentsUpdated 2026-10-21

In Malinké, balafon is not the name of a thing but of an action. The instrument is bala.

The name is a verb

Bala is the instrument, fô is to play it, and together they mean playing the bala. The English balafon is probably a European coinage, joining the Mandinka bála to fóo, to say, or to the Greek root phono. A verb, respelled as the name of an object.

In Manding the large bass is bala, with long keys and huge gourds; the small treble is balani, keys three or four centimetres long, carried on a strap, usually twenty-one of them. The Dagara, Lobi and Gurunsi call it gyil, Sierra Leone says balangi, the old Kingdom of Kongo had palaku — a similar name for another object in an unrelated circle.

Shaved wood, and a spider's egg case

The keys are béné wood, dried over a low flame and tuned by shaving: shave the middle and the note flattens, shave the end and it sharpens. Tuning here is subtraction.

The keys are tied to a frame with leather straps, and beneath hangs a graded row of calabashes, each with a small hole in its mouth covered by a thin membrane — originally the filament of a spider's egg case, today cigarette paper or thin plastic. Strike a key and the membrane moves, laying a continuous nasal hum under the note.

That membrane is the border between balafon and marimba. A marimba's gourds carry none and sound clean; the membraned branch has a buzz built in. A part that had no business being there became the family's identity card.

The same shape, invented twice

The balafon is believed to have developed independently of the instrument now called the marimba in southern Africa and South America. They arrived at nearly the same object without knowing of each other.

It also barely changes: oral history dates it to the twelfth-century Mali Empire, and seventeenth-century European visitors described instruments largely identical to today's.

Who may learn it, how it travels

The balafon is a griot instrument, inherited. The djembe is not, and that sentence defines the balafon by its opposite.

It has gone abroad three times. Through the slave trade, which carried players to the Americas: the Virginia Gazette recorded a barrafoo in 1776, and by the next century the references had vanished. As technique: in the 1980s the Rail Band led by Salif Keita was the best-known exponent, and Maninka players in eastern Guinea moved balafon phrasing onto imported guitars.

Sets of three are common in Guinea and Mali; Cameroon has a six-piece komenchang. In 2024 UNESCO listed the balafon, in the same batch as Indonesia's kolintang.

HS class

HS class: Idiophone · struck · wooden keys over gourd resonators · definite pitch, re-tunable.

同批列入，同一天被写进名录的两件乐器，一件在东南亚，一件在西非——名录本身也是一种跨文化的目录学）。音域：约 C3–C7，常用 C4–C6（按家族与音阶不同）
  • 对照线 ——kora：同为 griot 家传乐器，但科拉琴存的是「版本」（靠家族与记忆），巴拉风存的是「节奏型与调式」（靠师徒与村落）——两种家传乐器，一个存谱，一个存型；mbira：同为击鸣，但姆比拉无键无弦（靠指与共鸣片），巴拉风有键有音阶（靠有序的半音与调式）——一个把音高当作召唤的媒介，一个把音高当作可演奏的序列；marimba：形制几乎相同、起源相互独立（南方非洲与南美的马林巴 vs 西非的巴拉风）——人类的乐器史里，重复发明是常态；dunun：西方伪名 vs 动作名，两种「把动词当器物名」的错位方向相反；gong：中国锣靠金属体自身的余音，巴拉风靠葫芦腔与膜的耦合——两种把「延音」做到极致的办法：一个大而空，一个小而蒙；八音里的「锣」与这里的「葫芦膜」都是「让一个音在敲完之后继续活着」的两种方案
