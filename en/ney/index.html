The ney

A reed that was cut away — on the way back

Related

⭐⭐ The main line: a reed that was cut away, on the way back

「the plaintive sound of the reed flute to 'longing to be reunited with the reed bed from which it came' being analogous to 'man's yearning for union with God'.」

The 13th-century poet Rumi wrote of the flute that it and the human body are both 「hollow vessels needing the breadth of life to come alive」; ⭐⭐ the flute's plaintive tone is a longing to return to the reed bed from which it came — just as a human longs for union with God.

⭐⭐ The site's most famous instance of an instrument used as an existential metaphor —— not 「sad like a flute」, but the flute itself telling a story of separation (against the site's 「instrument as metaphor」 lineage: the atumpan is the king's tongue · the talk box's mouth is the instrument · the mbira is the ancestors' voice · the ney is an instrument that has been regretting its own severance ever since).

⭐⭐ The oldest thing on the site — and still alive

⭐⭐ 「depictions of ney players appearing in the wall paintings of the Egyptian pyramids and actual neys being found in the excavations at Ur」 —— ⭐⭐ played continuously for 4,500–5,000 years, 「one of the oldest musical instruments still in use」.

⭐⭐ Physical neys were dug out of Ur —— ⭐⭐ a working instrument lay in the ruins of a Sumerian city: the only instrument on the site where the archaeological site and the performance site are the same place (against the ocarina's archaeology, where hole count is the timeline · the mbira, invented twice in bamboo and iron —— the ney's archaeology: it is still alive).

⭐⭐ And it is a forerunner of the modern flute —— ⭐⭐ the source of today's flute family lies in the reed beds of Mesopotamia (against the oud, which exported westward —— one instrument sent out, one instrument that came from the west).

⭐⭐ Its name is its material: 「Ney is an old Persian word for reed from the Arundo donax plant」 —— ⭐⭐ the site's ninth naming pole: the plant (the material name oud for wood · the abstract concept qanun for rule · the plant name ney for the reed itself) —— ⭐⭐ the flute's name does not say what it is; it says what it used to be.

⭐⭐ A reed, cut, bored and bevelled

⭐ Form: a hollow stalk of Arundo donax · ⭐ nine segments articulated by eight nodes (Grinnell's specimen) · bored to a narrow cylindrical bore · one end ground to a bevelled edge (the Arabic form has no mouthpiece and is blown straight at the edge; the Turkish form has carried a wooden, horn, bone, ivory or plastic oblique mouthpiece, the başpare, since the 11th century) · six finger holes in front plus one thumbhole behind (six in the Persian form, seven in the Arabic and Turkish) · the Persian ney runs about 50–75 cm.

⭐⭐ How it is played: held obliquely down to the right, the bevelled edge resting on the lower lip; thumb and the first three fingers of the left hand cover the thumbhole and the upper three fingerholes.

⭐⭐ Each hole spans only a semitone to three quarter tones, and microtones come from three adjustments at once —— ⭐⭐ partly covering a hole, reshaping the oral cavity and lips, and altering the angle of the pipe against the mouth together with breath force —— ⭐⭐ this is the third way of reaching microtones: ⭐⭐ a hand (the oud) · a lever (the qanun) · the mouth and the breath (the ney) —— one maqam system, three civilisational answers, the oldest of them the most bodily.

⭐⭐ Seven pipes, one set: the Arabic system divides them by note — Rast (equivalent to C, the longest and lowest) · Dukah (D, the commonest) · Busalik (E) · Jaharka (F) · Nawa (G) · Hussayni (A) · Ajam (B or B♭); ⭐⭐ players normally carry the whole set in one case —— ⭐⭐ here a 「set」 means seven physical pipes in different keys (against the auto-tune's bank of presets).

⭐⭐ Low or high: a mark of occasion

⭐⭐ Pastors and scholars play different pipes: in the Arab world the ney is traditionally a pastoral instrument, favouring shorter pipes at higher pitch, while schools and religious settings use the low register, and the Sufi tradition studies the lowest registers; the Turks use longer neys —— ⭐⭐ 「lower or higher」 is not a timbral preference but a sign of the occasion (against vocal-production, where breath and occasion decide timbre · the ney: the occasion decides the pitch).

⭐⭐ Rumi and the Mevlevi: ⭐⭐ the flute leads the samā' ceremony of the Mevlevi Sufi order in Turkey; in Egypt and other Sufi brotherhoods a shorter 「kawala」 is used, together with the frame drum bendir.

⭐⭐ An instrument once written as an extension of the body: Rumi held that the flute's body and the human body are both hollow vessels —— ⭐⭐ in Middle Eastern poetry the flute's self-account and the person's self-account are the same thing.

⭐⭐ A purely gendered instrument: 「Traditionally an instrument played only by males」 — female players have only recently emerged in Turkey (against the dunun, traditionally male · the shekere, female —— the ney: once male only).

⭐⭐ A mistranslation waiting to happen: the plant Arundo donax is often rendered as a 「reed pipe」 in the reed-pipe sense —— ⭐⭐ because the English word reed covers both the plant and the vibrating tongue: the site's cleanest case of one English word's two meanings colliding in translation (against the site's naming series: the Western misnomer djun djun · the action name balafon · ney's reed is both the cane and the reed).

⭐ The kargı düdük: the Turkish long reed flute, legendarily connected with Alexander the Great, whose sound resembles the two-voice throat singing kargyra of the Turkic peoples (melody clear, the low drone barely audible) —— ⭐⭐ a hidden line from reed to two-voice singing.

⭐⭐ Renamed everywhere: gasba / guesba in the Maghreb; the narh of Sindh and the nel of Balochistan inherit its name and blowing style; ⭐⭐ in Romania the word nai denotes the pan flute —— an eastern end-blown flute became, next door, the name of a different shape.
