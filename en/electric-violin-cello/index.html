The electric violin and cello

The disappearing body — the resonance box dismantled piece by piece, the sound handed to electricity

Related

ViolinCelloElectric guitarThereminElectronic drumsTimbre

⭐⭐ The main line: the disappearing body

「the system captured only a narrow slice of the spectrum – roughly 100–2,500Hz — so much of the violin's overtone richness never reached the groove; what shimmered in a hall could become thin and ghostly on disc」.

⭐⭐ The electric violin's history is the resonance box being dismantled piece by piece:

EraFormWhat was removed
1899Stroh: membrane + hornthe wooden body's function, keeping its shape
1927–30pickups on wood / skeleton / bodilessthe need to 「sound by itself」
1935Rickenbacker's bakelite 「stick」the body's shape
1958Fender: hollow body + armature pickupa famous house's solitary attempt
1990s–piezo · MIDI · silentthe need to sound at all

⭐⭐ In the end the instrument can be a stick, a board, a frame — the disappearing body is not the instrument's death; it is an instrument turning from 「a thing」 into 「an interface」.

⭐⭐ The motive: the sound that couldn't be recorded

⭐⭐ The electric violin was not about being louder — it was about surviving — first recording (the wax cylinder's narrow band), then touring (「hearing acoustic instruments in stadiums was impossible」).

⭐⭐ Stroh 1899: a watchmaker built a violin for the recording horn — 「trained as a watchmaker in Frankfurt before settling in London, where he pivoted to electrical engineering」; 「the sound was concentrated into the recording horn in a way that a carved wooden body could not」; nine guineas in 1911 (a decent factory violin: two); Julio de Caro played it in Buenos Aires, where locals called it violín-corneta (cornet violin).

⭐⭐ Against Roland's watchmaker origin (Kakehashi): two clockmakers rewrote the destinies of two instrument families — the rhythm machine and the violin. And the Stroh violin lived about thirty years, then was retired by the electric microphone era it had helped open.

⭐⭐ The birth certificate is a pile of patents

⭐⭐ 「the majority of information regarding the origins of the electric violin can now be found in patents」 — a technology's childhood is often witnessed only by legal documents. (Against the site's name-vs-reality lineage: the talk box has three claimants; the electric violin's inventors are scattered across a stack of patents.)

⭐⭐ Fender 1958: a famous instrument that barely existed

「Leo Fender was thinking about creating an electric violin as early as 1948 — he planned to complete a set of electric stringed instruments」; 「the pickup was Fender's own invention: the bridge rocks → a metal pad (armature) vibrates → disturbs the magnetic field of a single-coil pickup — like a spark plug in a car engine」.

⭐⭐ Applied June 1958, debuted at Nashville's WSM show that October (sunburst · hollow maple body); yet 「as recently as May 2005, one of the world's leading specialists in vintage electric instruments had only ever seen two examples in over 40 years; Sotheby's, Christie's and Bonhams had never had one pass through their sales」 — ⭐⭐ a famous house, a famous instrument, almost nobody has seen it: the electric violin's 「ghost instrument」. (Against the site's warehouse-of-failures lineage — this one didn't fail; it was forgotten.)

⭐⭐ The electric cello, and the endpoint: silence

⭐⭐ Pfeil also envisioned a cello with a magnetic pickup; Stroh extended the design to viola, cello and even double bass — collectively the Stroviols. ⭐⭐ The modern echo: 「the similarity between the 1930 Makhonine electric violin and the Yamaha Silent Violin」 — sixty years apart, the same answer twice: take the body away, and the sound has freedom.

⭐⭐ One endpoint of electrification is 「silence」 — the silent violin: all the sound in the headphones, silence in the room (against the electric-drums entry: 「acoustic feel without the acoustic noise」 — the electric violin said it eighty years earlier). MIDI violins turn bow speed and pressure into MIDI; five-, six- and seven-string models widen the range — the electric violin's end is 「anything goes」: no standard form, because the body is gone.

⭐ A pair of de-bodiments

⭐⭐ Against the auto-tune entry — two de-bodiment routes completed in the same era: Auto-Tune removes the singer's body (pitch moved directly), the electric violin removes the instrument's body (resonance handed to pickup and speaker) — one lets 「singing」 need no throat, the other lets 「the violin」 need no body. (And the talk box's mouth filter is the reverse direction: putting the body back.)

The player lineage: Jean-Luc Ponty (fusion violin) · Mahavishnu Orchestra's Jerry Goodman · Tom Waits · They Might Be Giants (recorded 「I Can Hear You」 on a wax cylinder at the Edison Laboratory with a Stroh violin) · Shakira's tours.

  • ⭐⭐ 主线：琴身的消失（本页最该记住的一条） —— 电小提琴的历史是「共鸣箱被一块块拆掉」的历史：木琴身 → 金属膜 + 喇叭（Stroh 1899）→ 拾音器贴在木琴身上（Starzl 1927 · Stuff Smith）→ 骨架琴 / 无琴身棍（Pfeil 1928 · Makhonine 1930 · Rickenbacker 1935）→ 空心身 + 独特拾音（Fender 1958）→ 压电与 MIDI → 静音；⭐⭐ 每一次拆掉一块木头，「提琴」的定义就宽一分——到最后，琴可以只是一根棍、一块板、甚至一个可以戴在身上的框架。琴身的消失不是乐器的死亡，是乐器从「一件东西」变成「一个界面」
  • ⭐⭐ 动机：录不下的声音 —— 「从 1877 年声学时代黎明开始，录音会议要求乐手把声音灌进一只喇叭——系统只捕捉频谱的窄窄一条，大约 100–2,500Hz——小提琴的大量泛音 richness 从未到达沟槽」；「大厅里 shimmer 的，到唱片上变得又薄又幽灵」；⭐⭐ 电小提琴不是为了让提琴更响，是为了让提琴「留得下」——先是录音，后是球场与巡演（「在 stadium 里听原声乐器已无可能」）——一件乐器的电声化，动机往往不在音乐厅，在录音棚和体育场
  • ⭐⭐ Stroh 1899：钟表匠 + 喇叭（对照 Kakehashi） —— 「Johannes Matthias Augustus Stroh 在 Frankfurt 受钟表匠训练，后定居伦敦转攻电气工程；1899 年他专利了一只机械放大提琴，用膜片 + 金属号筒」；「声音被集中进录音 horn 的方式，是雕花的木身体做不到的」；「1911 年伦敦 Barnes & Mullins 卖九畿尼——当时一把像样的工厂琴两畿尼」；「1920 年代布宜诺斯艾利斯的 Julio de Caro 用它，当地人叫它 violín-corneta（短号提琴）」；「1920 年代后半唱片公司转向电麦克风录音后，Stroh 提琴变得稀少」；⭐⭐ 一位钟表匠造了「给录音喇叭的提琴」（对照 Roland 的钟表出身 Kakehashi）——而它活了三十年，然后被自己开启的时代淘汰
  • ⭐⭐ 电小提琴的出生证明是一堆专利 —— 「Elisha Gray 1874 年实验『提琴的电气化』——在小提琴上装金属板接收并放大电振荡——但在他的实验里，提琴扮演的是放大器（ Musical Telegraph 是世界第一件电乐器）」；「R.F. Starzl 1927：拾音器贴在 f 孔——已知最早的 electric-acoustic 提琴之一」；「Victor Pfeil 1928：骨架琴，电磁拾音器藏在琴马正下方」；「Ivan Makhonine 1930：法国《L'Illustration》报道 Cecilia Hansen 拉一把没有共鸣体、用电缆接放大器的琴」；「Rickenbacker Electro Violin 1935：George Beauchamp 设计，电木（后铝/木）『棍子』——极简设计证明了传统乐器形态改变的可能性」；「National Vio-Electric 1936：John Dopyera 开发，爵士小提琴家『Stuff』Smith 演奏——拾音器由两枚小磁线圈组成」；⭐⭐ 「多数关于电小提琴起源的信息现在只能在专利里找到」——一项技术的童年，常常只有法律文书作证
  • ⭐⭐ Fender 1958：一件几乎不存在的名器 —— 「Leo Fender 早在 1948 年就在想电小提琴——计划完成电弦乐全套（吉他、贝斯、曼陀林、提琴）」；「1958 年 6 月 2 日申请『electric violin and electromagnetic pickup therefor』；1958 年 10 月纳什维尔 WSM 展首发（sunburst 漆 · 空心枫木身 · bolt-on 琴颈 · Kluson 齿轮弦轴）」；「拾音器是 Fender 自己的发明：琴马 rocking → 金属 pad（armature）振动 → 扰动单线圈拾音器的磁场——像汽车引擎的火星塞；调节间隙 profoundly 影响输出（『大于 1/32 英寸，最好约 1/64 英寸』）」；「2005 年时，一位世界领先的 vintage 电乐器专家 40 年里只见过两把；苏富比/佳士得/邦瀚斯从未上拍过一把」；⭐⭐ 名门 + 名器 + 几乎无人见过——电小提琴史上的「幽灵名器」（对照全站「失败品仓库」谱系：这一件不是失败，是被忘记）
  • ⭐⭐ 电大提琴与现代 —— 「Pfeil 也构想了几件电弦乐器，包括一把 cello，用磁拾音」；「Stroh 把设计扩展到 viola、cello 甚至 double bass，统称 Stroviols」；⭐⭐ 现代对照：「Yamaha Silent Violin 与 1930 年 Makhonine 的无琴身提琴的相似」——六十年，同一个答案被两次给出：把琴身拿掉，声音就有了自由；MIDI 小提琴（Zeta 等）把弓速/压力变成 MIDI；五弦、六弦、七弦扩展音域——⭐⭐ 电提琴的尽头是「什么都行」：没有标准形制，因为琴身已经不在
  • ⭐⭐ 对照全站「去身体化」谱系（auto-tune 条） —— Auto-Tune 去掉了歌者的身体（音高被直接挪位），电小提琴去掉了琴的身体（共鸣被交给拾音器与音箱）——⭐⭐ 两条去身体化的路线在同一时代完成：一个让「唱」不再需要嗓子，一个让「提琴」不再需要琴身。而 talk box 条的口腔滤波提醒我们：也有反方向的——把身体接回去
