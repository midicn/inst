Rainstick

A shaken idiophone of internal spirals and grains — its shape of sound set by how fast you tilt it

1 min read34 characters条目PercussionUpdated 2026-09-26

Stand a rainstick upright, then tip it over slowly. A few grains fall, then a stream, then a few again — what you hear over the next minute is weather, not a note.

There is no pitch to speak of and nowhere to strike. The only variable in your hands is how fast you turn it.

The spiral is a flow limiter

The tube holds small stones, seeds or sand. Spines or baffles are set into the wall in a spiral, so the grains travel one channel at a time, and the fall from the first grain to the last has a definite duration.

The traditional build is the cheapest: a length of dried cactus, its spines pressed inward to form the spiral. Most are now wood or plastic with inserted baffles. Both aim at the same thing — keep the grains from arriving at once. Take the spiral out and you are left with one splash.

Slow tilt, quick flip

A slow vertical tilt gives a rain that rises and fades. A flip makes it urgent. A slight tilt leaves a few ticks.

Laid flat, it is silent. That matters more than it sounds: the player can stop it at any instant, which no struck instrument allows.

Quiet, but never buried

Its band is fine broadband noise at a modest level. In a quiet passage it sounds like weather outside the window; inside a loud tutti it vanishes. Layered with wind chimes and the gong, it is a familiar atmosphere bed in film scoring. None of the three keeps time; they supply a place.

From rain ritual to the scoring stage

Dried-cactus rainsticks were already part of rain-making ritual in South America, in what is now Chile and Peru. Late in the twentieth century, world and film music took it up as a colour; wooden and plastic versions followed, and it became a children's instrument too. The same sound, moved from altar to sound effect.

What moves inside is grain, not water — water cannot be made to descend a spiral in controlled steps. Knowing that, the rain you hear is gravity, slowed by a piece of geometry.

HS class

HS class: Idiophone · shaken · unpitched · Bayin: not applicable, the eight categories are a Chinese system the rainstick falls outside.
