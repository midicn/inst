Saer

One string sings; a dozen more add the colour

Related

AijiekeErhuMatouqinSihuRegisterTimbre

Across the eleven bowed strings written so far, the names mostly match the things. This one does not: the "setar" in the Qing court documents is not the saer of today.

「《清史稿》所载『塞他尔』……⭐ 主奏弦为两根丝弦，且"以手冒拨指，或木拨弹丝弦、应钢弦取声"，属拨弹类乐器。可见其与当代萨塔尔为『同名异实』的两种乐器。」

⭐⭐ Main line 59: same name, different thing — the Qing "setar" was a plucked instrument; today's saer is bowed.

Same name. Entirely two different objects.

⭐⭐ One string sings; a dozen more add the colour

How it makes sound comes first, because here is what is unusual:

「上拴一根金属主奏弦和九至十三根金属共鸣弦……⭐ 马尾弓置于弦外拉奏，只拉主奏弦，不拉共鸣弦，共鸣弦只起加强共鸣和美化音色作用。」

⭐⭐ The bow plays exactly one string. The other dozen or so are never touched; they only make the sound prettier. And how those resonance strings are tuned is the crucial technical point on this page:

⭐⭐ This is the second time in the whole site a row of un-stopped strings appears — but the two instruments divide the work in opposite ways. The aijieke's resonance strings are players: each has a pitch and can carry a line. The saer's are engineers: they tune the colour. ⭐ Both come out of the same 回部乐 tradition, so neither is the first to invent the idea.

⭐⭐ Eighteen frets plus five high ones — a fingerboard designed for modulation

The most practical passage on this page:

「指板上以丝弦缠就 18 个品位，⭐ 面板上另粘有 5 个高音木质品位……⭐ 按十二平均律排列，能演奏各调乐曲。」

⭐⭐ There is one melody string and its pitch is fixed（often between B and d, commonly c）， yet the fingerboard carries a full set of frets laid out in twelve-tone temperament. ⭐⭐ So the saer moves between keys without moving the bridge and without restringing. The others achieve this by having more strings（the sihu has four; the matouqin has five registers）. The saer takes the other road: few strings, complete fingerboard. And the frets are not all on the neck: ⭐ five more sit on the soundboard itself — ⭐ the neck covers the middle and low, the soundboard supplies the top. Rare among Chinese bowed strings.

⭐⭐ "the bridge's right side is raised so the string stands away from the fingerboard" is the key one: ⭐ the left hand stops frets while the string itself is not choked — that is what keeps the tone clear rather than muffled.

⭐⭐ Three registers, three characters

Most instruments in this section say only "the tone is bright". The saer's registers are written out:

「⭐ 高音区音色尖锐，略带金属声；中音区音色明亮优美；低音区音色浑厚，略带沙哑声，声音富有层次。」

⭐⭐ "close to the human voice" is the key to this instrument. It was not built as background — it was designed to take the place of a voice inside the song. That is why the left hand carries so many vocal techniques（揉 · 滑 · 颤 · 带）: ⭐ however a voice makes its breath, the string must make its breath. And this explains its standing: ⭐⭐ the lead instrument of the muqam ensemble（not an accompaniment）— specifically, 「十二木卡姆演奏中『琼乃额曼』的『木凯迪满』部分往往由萨塔尔琴来演奏」, found in 「喀什 · 和田 · 莎车 · 库车」.

⭐ The family of a name: one word, three instruments

Back to that "same name, different thing" — it points at a larger family:

⭐ 「考『塞依吐尔』『塞他尔』『萨塔尔』当为同一名词的不同音译。」

⭐⭐ All three are different transliterations of one word — and they belong to two different families:

NamePeopleClass
萨塔尔 / saerUyghur⭐ bowed（today's instrument）
弹布尔Uyghur · Uzbek⭐ plucked
塞依吐尔Tajik⭐ plucked（⭐「虽有五根弦，却被组合成了三组」）

⭐⭐ The Qing court's "setar"（plucked） and today's "satar"（bowed） are two things wearing one name. That is why the sources say 「其形制近似弹布尔」— ⭐ of course they resemble each other: same source, one of them was later turned into a bowed instrument. ⚠️ Both documents place it among the plucked in the Qing（「以手冒拨指…应钢弦取声」）, so this page treats it as evidence of a homonym, not a problem to reconcile. ⭐ And a legend explains the instrument's standing:

「相传远古时候，安拉用泥巴捏成人，让灵魂进入人体时，灵魂不愿进入黑暗的人体，安拉便制作了一把萨塔尔置于人体……灵魂听到这动听的音乐，便情不自禁地跳着萨玛舞，进入了人体。」

⭐⭐ In this legend the instrument has exactly one job: to call the soul in. History: said to have circulated in Kashgar by the 14th–15th centuries, later spreading north and east; listed in the Qing court's 回部乐; ⭐ the《律吕正义后编》records「司二弦一人，回名塞他尔」; ⚠️ another account says its first documentary appearance is in the Qianlong-era《钦定四库全书》; it reached Yining in the 1950s. Playing: the body is held upright on the left thigh, the right hand bows outside the strings, the left stops frets; ⭐ the left hand leads with the index finger, joined by the middle, ring and little; the right uses 长弓 · 短弓 · 连弓 · 顿弓 · 颤弓; ⭐「右手持弓方法与艾捷克相同」.

  • ⭐⭐ 音色（三个音区分开写，这在本段很少见）：「⭐ 高音区音色尖锐，略带金属声；中音区音色明亮优美；低音区音色浑厚，略带沙哖声，声音富有层次」；另记「音色圆润，接近人声」「音色明亮」→ ⭐⭐ 「接近人声」是理解萨它尔的钥匙 —— 它不是背景音，被设计成能替代人声进入唱腔——所以它左手有那么多揉弦技法（揉 · 滑 · 颤 · 带），因为人声是怎么出气的，弦就得怎么出气。而这一段也解释了它的地位：十二木卡姆乐队的主奏乐器（不是伴奏），具体到段落——「十二木卡姆演奏中『琳乃额曼」的『木凯迪满」部分往往由萨塔尔琴来演奏」；分布「喀什 · 和田 · 莎车 · 库车」等地。依方志类资料

  • ⭐ 演奏法：演奏时竖置琴身于左腿之上，右手持马尾弓在弦外擦弦发声，左手按品位改变音高；⭐ 左手按弦以食指为主，兼用中指 · 无名指 · 小指，技法有揉 · 滑 · 颤 · 带等多种；右手常用连弓 · 分弓和顿弓，另有长弓 · 短弓 · 颤弓；⭐「右手持弓方法与艾捷克相同」；另有打音 · 擞音 · 泛音依百科与地方志类资料

  • ⭐⭐ 同一个词的三件乐器：萨塔尔 / 萨它尔（维吾尔族 · ⭐弓弦）｜弹布尔（维吾尔 · 乌兹别克族 · ⭐拨弦）｜塞依吐尔（塔吉克族 · ⭐拨弦，「虽有五根弦，却被组合成了三组」）→ ⭐⭐ 清代宫廷那个「塞他尔」（拨弦），和今天这个「萨塔尔」（弓弦），是同一个名字的两种东西。所以「其形制近似弹布尔」这句话现在就好懂了：它们本来就该像——同源，只是后来一个被改成了弓弦乐器
