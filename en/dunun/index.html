The dunun (Mande bass drums)

One word, two instruments — "dunun" is both this drum and the category "drum"

1 min read394 characters条目World InstrumentsUpdated 2026-10-21

In Manding, dunun is not the name of an instrument. It is the ordinary word for the category "drum". To name one drum you add a size: dundunba, sangban, kenkeni.

Namingdunun = the Manding word for "drum"; dundunba large · sangban middle · kenkeni small

One word, two instruments

This is where the trouble starts. The Yoruba also have a drum called dùndún, an hourglass-shaped talking drum. Transliterated, the names look almost the same; the instruments have nothing to do with each other.

One layer further out is a name the West invented. "Djun djun" has been in catalogues for a long time, and there is no such word in Malinké. A word that does not exist has been in use for a century.

Three sizes, three parts

Ba means "big" in Malinké, so dundunba is literally "big dunun". The three drums line up by size and by register: the kenkeni, smallest and highest, holds the pulse; the sangban in the middle plays the melodic pattern that defines the rhythm; the dundunba, largest and lowest, lays down sparse, deep notes.

They are not three substitutes for a bass register; they are three parts. Nor does the melody belong to any one of them — the patterns interlock and the melody grows between them.

One player with three drums, or three with one

There are two answers. In the Mandingue style each player takes one drum, laid on its side, striking the head with a stick in the right hand and the kenken bell with the left. In the ballet style one player commands all three drums standing on the floor.

The bell deserves a mention: it sits on top of the drum and is struck by the left hand, so metal rings over skin. A percussion instrument that carries its own bell is not common in West Africa.

It used to carry messages

The dunun once announced local rulers and public affairs. The job later passed to the dedicated talking drum, and the dunun went to work as a foundation — instruments change trades.

The name of a rhythm is also the name of an occasion. Kuku, Sunu, Soli, Kassa each belong to harvest, initiation, marriage, a caste celebration. Asking what a rhythm is called is asking what event this is.

Its road abroad runs through schools. Mamady Keïta's Tam Tam Mandingue has branches in many countries, and Famoudou Konaté and Mohamed Diaby teach around the world. An organisation that teaches outlives one that tours.
