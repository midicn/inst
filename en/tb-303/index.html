The TB-303

A monophonic bass synthesizer and step sequencer, with accent, slide and filtering central to acid patterns

1 min readElectronicUpdated 2026-11-02

instrumentelectronicelectrophonewesternanalog-synthtb-303

Related

HS class: electrophone; oscillation produces a signal that is filtered and amplified.

Roland introduced the TB-303 Bass Line in 1981 as a programmable bass accompaniment machine. Musicians later used its step sequencing, slide, accent and resonant filtering in dance music. That change of use need not be reduced to one accidental operation inventing a whole style.

Sound generation and sequencing

PartRoleComparison
OscillatorSupplies the initial waveformHold notes and filter settings fixed
FilterShapes the spectrum and resonanceChange cutoff or resonance separately
EnvelopeShapes the movement of each noteBegin with sparse notes
SequencerArranges pitches, durations, rests, accents and slidesAdd one feature at a time to a short loop

The original is monophonic. Slide connects pitches, while accent participates in the sound and envelope behaviour. Neither maps perfectly to an ordinary MIDI tie or volume value. Emulations and modified units may add further controls.

Write a short pattern first

Use two or three pitches, add rests and check the relationship to the drums. Retain a version without slides, then introduce slides at selected positions. Add only a few accents and listen for a clear hierarchy. More notes do not automatically create more movement.

Check the production chain

Compare the same pattern on your instrument or emulation and record waveform, tuning, filtering, envelope, accent and slide settings. Watch level changes during filter movement and judge the bass in the arrangement. The MIDI examples show note data rather than original-machine recordings. Use a documented recording or the instrument itself to evaluate particular hardware.
