Yueqin (moon lute)

Shaped like a full moon — and the year it was finally declared its own instrument

2 min readChinese PluckedUpdated 2026-09-27

The body is a little over a foot long and round as a drum. The player flicks a plectrum held between thumb and forefinger, and the sound cuts through an opera band.

Round by name, short by temper

The body is a true circle. Soundboard and back are thin paulownia, for lightness and response; rim and neck are rosewood or similar, for stiffness. The name is drawn on the shape: round as the moon, voiced like a qin.

The neck is short, with eight or nine frets. That squat build favours the high register, exactly what opera bands want. Four strings run in pairs tuned a fifth apart and are struck with a plectrum; half the technique sharpens the grain.

Only an instrument since 1950

The Song treatise Yue Shu describes a round body, long neck, four strings and thirteen frets, and ends by crediting Ruan Xian: it was filed under the ruan. In the Qing the handle shrank into a short neck, and the name still followed.

Not until 1950 was the short-neck yueqin formally declared an instrument of its own. The body split first; the status arrived a century later. Borders differ too: Taiwan, Japan and Vietnam still call the long-neck type a yueqin.

Two reforms followed: wide-neck models reached eighteen to twenty-four frets in equal temperament and transposed freely; the 1980s long-neck model has thirty frets and extra beams.

Range

Two octaves in the common form, d1 to d3. Not loud, but it cuts: short neck, high register, plectrum, points not sheets. Wide-neck models reach more than three octaves, enough to be a band's treble voice.

Opera stage and mountain village

In Peking opera the yueqin, the jinghu and the second fiddle are the three core instruments. Before the second fiddle arrived the trio was jinghu, yueqin and sanxian; later four shared the work, an addition rather than a subtraction.

Peking opera often gives it a single string. It is not there for harmony but for a few bright points placed at the joints of a phrase.

Villages use it too. Yi dancers court with it, Bai opera accompanies with it, Dong ensembles pair it with the sheng, and Sichuan storytelling calls the act singing the yueqin.

On stage it must be clear; in the mountains, understood.

  • 从阮到月琴（分家史）：北宋陈旸《乐书》（卷一四一）：「月琴，形圆项长，上按四弦十三品柱，象琴之徽，转轸应律，晋阮咸造也」；「当时月琴的形制与阮相近，后来逐渐变化。到了清代，琴杆缩短为琴颈，演变为现代的式样」；「从宋朝起（月琴）作为乐器『阮琴』的异名，从清朝开始逐渐和阮琴分家，短颈月琴于 1950 年正式被确立为一种独立的乐器」；「清朝时期出现八品左右的短颈月琴，之后月琴就越来越与阮琴差别化，因其短胖的构造而特别适合演奏高音，常用于戏曲曲艺的伴奏」；「由于缺乏对月琴的官方定义，所以理应归类为阮琴的『长颈月琴』在中国中国台湾 · 日本和越南也能被算作是月琴」（引文按本站规范写法）依古籍与乐器史资料
