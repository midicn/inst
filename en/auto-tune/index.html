Auto-Tune

Correction pushed to its extreme becomes style — a seismic algorithm that found oil, then found pitch

An algorithm that hunted oil

At a lunch during the 1996 NAMM show someone asked what still needed inventing, and a neighbour joked: a box that would let me sing in tune. Andy Hildebrand heard it as a problem. He had spent years reading seismic waves with autocorrelation to locate drilling sites, and he played flute, a studio musician from sixteen. In 1989 he left oil for composition at Rice; in 1990 he founded Jupiter Systems. The same models, pointed at audio, could find pitch. Auto-Tune shipped in 1997.

One knob

Detect the pitch of a recorded voice, compare it with a chosen scale, shift it to the nearest correct note. Retune speed decides everything. Slow, and a note glides into tune almost inaudibly, which is studio routine. At zero, every note snaps onto the semitone grid and the voice turns into a staircase.

A graphical mode still allows note-by-note drawing of the pitch curve, and formant preservation keeps the resonance from travelling with the pitch, so the singer does not become someone else.

What you can hear

In 1998 Cher's Believe used an extreme, plainly audible setting. Asked about it, the producers called it a vocoder effect; the label thought it overdone and Cher said they could change that part over her dead body. The single reached number one in twenty-three countries. Hildebrand later admitted he had almost left the feature out.

In 2003 T-Pain heard Auto-Tune on a remix and decided he needed that sound as his whole identity. In 2008 Kanye West sang an entire album of heartbreak through it. The difference between T-Pain and a mainstream pop record was never whether the tool was there, but whether you could hear it. From 1997 to 2003 it was invisible, from 2003 to 2012 it was a style, and after 2012 it went invisible again; the question became how much.

It corrects pitch, not feeling

In 2023 the Recording Academy gave Hildebrand a Technical Grammy. The argument continues: if the pitch was moved electronically, is she singing in tune? The other side has a point — a recording was never a transcript of a live performance. Country and Americana want the unprocessed voice most loudly while correction is everywhere, and the best producers work at the level of the phrase.

In HS class terms Auto-Tune sits at the effects end of the electrophones: no sound of its own, parasitic on a voice, doing pitch detection and snap correction.

  • 主线：修正到极致就是风格（本页最该记住的一条） —— 「Auto-Tune 由 Antares Audio Technologies 于 1997 年发布，最初目的是透明的音高修正：修正录音表演里偏平或偏锐的音，让最终录音听起来像歌手从头到尾都唱准了」；三阶段：「1997–2003 透明修正期（工程师悄悄用，听众不知道它存在）」→「2003–2012 风格效果期（T-Pain · Kanye · Young Jeezy 把重度修正当作刻意的美学选择）」→「2012 至今无处不在的不可见期（音高修正是如此普遍，『问题不再是是否修正，而是修多少、什么风格』）」——一个工具的三个时代：从「隐形的」到「显性的」再到「隐形的」——音高从「事实」变成「参数」
  • 发明人：从地心到喉咙的算法 —— 「Andy Hildebrand 是地球物理工程师与数学家，在石油工业用自相关算法分析地震波绘制潜在钻探点」；「他也是一位长笛演奏家，16 岁起做录音棚乐手——1989 年离开石油业进入 Rice 大学 Shepherd School of Music 学作曲」；「1990 年创立 Jupiter Systems（Antares 前身）」；突破来自一个玩笑：「在 NAMM 展的一次午餐上，Hildebrand 问『什么需要被发明？』有人回答：『一个让我能唱准的盒子！』——这是个笑话，但他的齿轮开始转动」；「他发现把这些模型应用到音频，它们能检测音高」——地下找油的算法，被用来「修正」人声——一个从地心到喉咙的算法（对照声码器的「电信算法进音乐」：两次，算法都从工程世界越界到音乐世界）
  • Cher《Believe》（1998）：一个差点没放进软件的功能 —— 「制作人尝试了一个激进的新把戏：对 Cher 的声音施加极端的、高度可闻的 Auto-Tune 效果——有人问起时他们说是『声码器效果』」；「面对厂牌对过度使用的反对，Cher 说：『You can change that part of it over my dead body!』」；「Believe 成为 23 国冠军单曲，1998 年最畅销单曲」；Hildebrand：『I almost didn't put that feature in the software.   • 原理：一个旋钮写历史 —— 「分析录音人声的音高、与用户定义的音阶或调比较、把信号移到最近正确音——参数有速度、retune time 与人性化」；「低修正速度下音符自然滑入调（几乎不可察觉）；高修正速度下瞬间吸附目标音高——产生与风格性使用关联的机器人效果」；「Retune Speed = 0 ms——这一个设置把『微妙的音高修正』与『听起来像机器人』分开。一个旋钮。二十年的音乐史」；另有 graphical mode（逐音手动编辑音高曲线）与 formant 保持（保共振峰不伤音色）

  • 竞争者 Melodyne —— 「Auto-Tune 与其竞争者（特别是 Melodyne）通过分析录音人声的音高、把单个音符移向最近的半音目标来工作」——一个实时修正，一个离线直接编辑（DNA 级音高编辑）——「改数字」与「画数字」两种哲学
