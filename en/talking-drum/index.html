The talking drum

It cannot speak consonants, so it invented poetry — an instrument that talks in pitch

Related

The dunun (Mande bass drums)The djembeThe mbira (thumb piano)VoicePitch

⭐⭐ The main line: it cannot speak consonants, so it invented poetry

「can be used as a form of speech surrogacy by regulating its pitch and rhythm to mimic the tone and prosody of human speech」

The talking drum is an hourglass drum with two heads joined by leather tension cords, its waist trapped between the player's arm and body — the pitch changes by squeezing those cords, tightening the skin. ⭐⭐ This is not a metaphor: it talks in pitch.

⭐⭐ And its ability to talk rests on tonal languages: "In English, pitch conveys emotion……but in a tonal language, pitch determines the definition of the word itself. For example, in Yoruba, the syllable 'jo' can mean 'to dance,' 'to resemble,' or 'please,' depending entirely on whether it is spoken with a high, mid, or low tone." ⭐⭐ In English pitch is emotion; in Yoruba pitch is the word's definition — so the drum had to learn tones, not words. Three pressure levels give three pitches (Do arm fully relaxed · Re slight pressure · Mi cords squeezed tight) — ⭐⭐ an instrument saying three different meanings with three discrete settings.

⭐⭐ The finest passage in the site's library: a limit that produced rhetoric

「To make up for the lack of consonants, drummers would expand simple messages into longer, descriptive phrases. For instance, 'Come back home' might become: 'Make your feet come back the way they went, make your legs come back the way they went...'」

⭐⭐ It cannot produce consonants or vowels — so "come back home" is spoken as "make your feet come back the way they went, make your legs come back the way they went". ⭐⭐ The site's finest flaw-into-signature: not a better timbre, but an invented rhetoric — the limit forces a whole art of describing instead of naming, and that art becomes poetry. (Compare the site: the TB-303's sawtooth, the 808's transistors, the mbira's sympathetic tines — this one turns a flaw into a language.)

⭐⭐ And it has a dictionary that can die: "each word had a set phrase that had to be memorized. If a phrase fell out of use, it could be forgotten, leading to confusion." ⭐⭐ Harsher than the kora's "editions live in the player": here the drummer maintains a whole language, and a language can disappear整段 (compare the kora's jali holding history — here it is grammar itself).

⭐⭐ Faster than a horse: the jungle telegraph, and the ban

Eighteenth-century European observers recorded that messages travelled faster than a rider; the talking drum could carry news 20 miles (32 km), relayed by the next drummer — the "jungle telegraph".

⭐⭐ An extreme in the site's transmission history: from a spoken phrase to a letter to the telegraph to radio, the talking drum is the optimal solution before the telegraph — and its carrier is a human arm. ⭐⭐ And it was banned precisely because it worked: "the onslaught of colonialism enforced an involuntary hiatus on this tradition, as the use of talking drums for communication was prohibited", and in colonial conflicts "distinct efforts were sometimes made to ban or confiscate the drums to prevent organized resistance" — ⭐⭐ the site's most typical case of a technology negated by politics (compare the Telharmonium's wires in 1906, the 808's internal resistance at Roland, the tape's commercial obstruction at EMI — a tool that carries words is inherently a target). ⭐ And after the ban the practice "later blossomed and persists to date".

⭐⭐ The same object with a dozen native names — every people named it for themselves (contrast the dunun, which is one generic word plus local variants — the talking drum is the reverse). ⭐⭐ A third namesake case: the Dunan, Sangban, Kenkeni and Fontomfrom of the Yoruba system are other, non-hourglass drums — sharing names with the Mande dunun trio while being different instruments. ⭐⭐ And "dùndún" means "sweet sound" in Yoruba.

⭐⭐ The mother drum, the drummer, and the surname

⭐ The talking drum is a family: ìyáàlù (mother drum, the lead) · ìsáájú · kèrìkèrì · gúdúgúdú · ìkẹ́yìn — all hourglass pressure drums except the gúdúgúdú. ⭐⭐ A "mother drum" is a lead system (compare the djembe's lead djembe — both drum families are "one leader plus accompaniment").

⭐⭐ Drummerhood is written into the surname: "Ayan" means drummer, Ayangalu is believed to be the first Yoruba drummer (and the spirit or muse of drummers), so Yoruba surnames commonly carry the Ayan- prefix (Ayangbade, Ayantunde, Ayanwande). ⭐⭐ Harder than the kora's inherited jali: not only must you be born to it, you are named for it.

⭐ Origins: the Bono and Yoruba peoples, the Ghana Empire and the Hausa; first prominent at the twelfth-century inauguration rites of the Alaafin of Oyo; the royal instrument of Oyo and Ghana, where kings (Obas) kept drummers as their mouthpiece. ⭐ The construction is confined to modern West Africa (exceptions: northern Cameroon, western Chad and shared border populations). ⭐ The Serer tama is the fourth drum of the Woong / Xaat tradition (the dance of uncircumcised boys). ⭐ Today: juju music (King Sunny Ade's 1982 "Juju Music" and its multi-drummer trance jams) · Sikiru Adepoju (with Mickey Hart's Planet Drum and Carlos Santana) · Aralola "Ara" Olamuyiwa (with Stevie Wonder) · Francis Awe (a Yoruba prince who founded the Nigerian Talking Drum Ensemble).

⭐⭐ Closing: the mechanical twin of the human voice

Compare the site at large: the voice is the only instrument that carries meaning — the talking drum is its mechanical twin: it does not produce meaning, it carries other people's meaning.

⭐⭐ Three instruments on one line: the mbira uses pitch to summon the ancestors, the kora uses pitch to keep a genealogy, the talking drum uses pitch to deliver words — ⭐⭐ in three cultures pitch became respectively the supernatural, the archive and the language. ⭐⭐ And the site's extreme in speed sits here too: one sentence relayed across twenty miles by a chain of arms, faster than a horse (compare the transmission lineage: the Telharmonium wanted to put music on wires, the talking drum carried words on drumbeats, then telegraph, telephone, radio — the talking drum is the optimal solution before the telegraph).
