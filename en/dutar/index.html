Dutar

A name that says "two strings" — and three ways to divide a lute's strings

Related

PitchTimbreTanburRawapPipa

Its name is its spec sheet: dutar — "'du' means two, 'tar' means string: the instrument with two strings."

⚠️ And the authoritative dictionary adds one sentence: "The name comes from Persian, meaning 'two strings' — but some kinds of dutar have more than two." — the name recorded what it was; the instrument grew beyond it.

⭐⭐ Rule ㊼: strings and their jobs (part three): three Xinjiang lutes, three divisions.

One string leads, one holds the floor

⭐ Three points:

1. Two strings, two jobs. "Only the first string is stopped; the second serves as a drone" — the second string is its "floor" (on some types it is stopped too, usually with the thumb). ⭐ With this the trio completes: rawap "one versus many," tanbur "grouping," dutar "melody plus floor." 2. No sound holes. "No sound holes in the face; the cavity is the key part" — the sound leaves the box, not a hole. 3. It is also a craft object. Inlaid ox bone and horn, with pyrographed patterns — as with the rawap: "both an instrument and a craft object."

The name is the spec sheet — and spec sheets go stale

"du (two) ＋ tar (string) = the two-stringed instrument" — the most honest name in this batch: it states its own specification.

⚠️ But it also warns of something:

  • the dictionary: "meaning 'two strings' — but some kinds have more than two";
  • and on the tanbur page, the Qing statutes record "four iron strings," while today's instrument has five.

⭐ Hence names-history item ㉒: names and documents stop at the "most typical" form — while the instrument walks on. (Compare ㊶, "classification lags behind sound": one is *recognition* arriving late; this is the *name* stopping early.)

Range

"No dutar, no feast"

⭐ This proverb is its job description:

"Without a dutar there is no feast."

  • it is "the most widespread and most used plucked instrument in Uyghur society," called the "mother of Uyghur instruments";
  • used for family amusement, weddings, festivals, and as an accompanist in the Twelve Muqam;
  • ⭐ and its most characteristic use is self-accompanied singing: "the right hand uses all five fingers, none dispensable… small in sound but soft in tone, so the player can pour out feeling through it — which is why Uyghur women especially love to sing with the dutar in their own hands." A quiet instrument, and the one most often picked up at home.

⭐ The artist who carried this art furthest is Sanubar Tursun: from a musical family in Ili, trained in yangqin and composition, more than ten albums and 90-plus compositions — and she set Tagore, Yesenin and Abai to Uyghur song: dutar singing grew from the parlour into a transcultural dialogue of poetry and music.

⚠️ And its lineage: it is held to descend from the ancient tanbūr of Khorasan; on the murals of the Kizil and Kumtura caves "you can see its slender silhouette" — its history is painted on walls and played in hands.

Common misconceptions

  • "Two strings is too few." ⚠️ One stops for melody, one drones; with 16–18 frets and fourths/fifths tuning it carries a whole song alone — and it is the most widespread plucked instrument among the Uyghur.
  • "It must be loud." ⚠️ The opposite: "small in sound but soft in tone" — its setting is the household feast, not a concert hall; which is exactly why it became the instrument of self-accompanied singing.
  • "The dutar is the tanbur." ⚠️ Similar build (both ladle-like), different strings: two versus five in three groups; and the dutar's face has no sound holes.
  • "The name says two strings, so it has two." ⚠️ The sources state plainly: "some kinds have more than two." The name is its origin, not its boundary.
  • "It is only an instrument." ⚠️ It is also a craft object — bone and horn inlay, pyrogravure, "simple, ancient, elegant."

Timbre, and how to hear it

Three cues:

1. Hear the floor. The drone string never stops: the melody walks on top, the drone holds below. 2. Hear "five fingers." "All five fingers, none dispensable" — a busy technique, a small sound: busy in the hand, quiet in the ear. 3. Hear the room. It is not a concert-hall instrument but a feast-and-household one — when you hear it, picture the room after dinner.
