The bawu shares its reed, its land and its tone with the hulusi — the difference is one sentence:

the hulusi sounds several notes (it carries its own backup); the bawu sounds one (it sings alone). And its name and its legends are better than its mechanism: "Bawu" = the *ba* of "Bachong" ＋ the *wu* of "Meiwu" — two lovers' names, spliced head to tail, became an instrument.

⚠️ Better still: one legend tells its own structure — the girl's tongue is cut out; a magic bird brings her tongue and a length of bamboo, saying: "The bamboo will help you speak." ⭐⭐ Rule ㉞: the legend gives away the structure — the bawu's very heart is called the "reed tongue." The tongue that went into the bamboo in the story is the triangular bronze reed in the object; from "cannot speak" to "speaks through a pipe" — in the bawu, "the talking instrument" is not a metaphor but literal.

One bamboo, one bronze reed

Three points:

1. One bamboo is the whole body. A pipe about 26–28 cm long: top closed by a node or plug, bottom open — and that one-ended shape is exactly the cavity a reed needs to couple with. 2. A triangular bronze reed, set near the top node. Tiny and thin, it is the instrument's heart — "the airflow sets the reed's tongue vibrating and excites the air column." ⭐ Keep that word: reed tongue (簧舌) — the next section needs it. 3. ⭐ Two ways to hold it, two gauges. "Short and thin, blown vertically; thick and long, blown across": vertically, the lips cover the reed; across, the reed sits between the lips. The small pipe is the common one (treble/mid, 30–50 cm); the bass bawu (60–80 cm) is carried sideways.

Three origins

Three stories, explaining three things:

LegendWhoWhat it explains
Hani · loversMeiwu ＋ Bachong⭐ the name: head of one, tail of the other → *Bawu*
Hani · siblingsMolong and Mopo⭐ the purpose: saying the unsayable → "matchmaker instrument"
Yi Nesu · deaf-muteunnamed⭐ the structure: a worm-holed bamboo sounds → cut holes → *feili*

⭐ And the first legend holds the line worth keeping — the bird tells the tongueless girl: "The bamboo will help you speak." Now note the coincidence: the bawu's reed is called, in technical Chinese, the "reed *tongue*." The story's tongue entered a bamboo; the object's "reed tongue" is set into a bamboo. A story told to children spells out the mechanics of an instrument. (This site's observation: the legend's three layers — name, purpose, structure — are exactly the three doors into understanding any instrument.)

The name: spliced from two people

The bawu is this batch's first person-named instrument — (names-history item ⑭: named after people — its companion being the ruan, named for Ruan Xian who played it.)

Range

From the hills to the stage

  • It used to talk. Young men "play love songs on the bawu, talking to the girls"; Hani elders and youths carry it everywhere. The Yi even love "two bawus in the same key, in unison or in dialogue" — a one-note instrument borrowing a second player to hold a conversation.
  • It sits in bands. In Yi song-and-dance gatherings along the south bank of the Red River, the bawu plays alongside dizi, small sanxian, yueqin and leaf-whistles — a whole village orchestra on its own.
  • Since the 1950s it has been re-fitted. Makers developed the keyed bawu and the double bawu: the keyed one "modulates into four keys" with a wider range and more power; the double one pairs two identical pipes, brighter and louder. The reformed bawu "can play not only traditional regional pieces but transplanted foreign music — Japan's *Kitano Haru* among others."
  • ⭐ And so it left the hills for the stage — English-language sources say its sound is instantly recognisable in film scores such as *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon*, laying a southwestern undercoat beneath Western orchestration.

Common misconceptions

  • "The bawu is just the hulusi." Same technology, different instrument: one pipe → one note versus the hulusi's several; the bawu's reed is bare, the hulusi's hides in its gourd; the ranges are "usually the same," but one sings alone and the other keeps backup.
  • "Its 'talking' is a figure of speech." ⚠️ In the legend it is literal — a tongueless girl, a bamboo that speaks for her; and the reed's technical name really is "reed tongue."
  • "The name is a transliteration." The Chinese name "bawu" is spliced from two people's names (Bachong · Meiwu); the ethnic names (meiba, bilu, gebi…) are all different again.
  • "One pipe cannot do two parts." ⚠️ The Yi tradition of "two bawus, unison or dialogue" says otherwise — the second voice comes from a second person.
  • "It is a single-reed instrument." The same terminology trap as the hulusi: that phrase means "one reed per pipe"; by classification it stands with the sheng among free reeds.

Timbre, and how to hear it

Three cues:

1. Hear the hazy brightness. One pipe makes it more direct and closer than the hulusi, but the reed's faint rasp remains — the "weeping" undercoat. 2. Hear the speech-rhythm. Taps, vibrato and slides are its tone of voice: a bawu phrase rises and falls like a sentence — it talks rather than sings. 3. Hear the pair. If two bawus answer each other high and low, that is the Yi dialogue: one instrument's way to hold a conversation is to find a second player.

  • ⭐⭐ 近现代改革：「20 世纪 50 年代以来，音乐工作者对巴乌进行了一系列改良，研制出了加键巴乌和双管巴乌。加键巴乌扩大了音域和音量，能转四个调；双管巴乌则由两支形制 · 音高完全相同的巴乌并排组成，音域更宽，音色清脆明亮，音量也更大」；改革后的巴乌「还能调微……能奏出各种滑音 · 打音 · 吐音 · 颤音 · 飞指 · 抹音等技巧音」，「不仅可以演奏传统的地方风格曲目，还可移植外国乐曲，如日本的《北国之春》等」依乐器资料与云南乐器资料
  • ⭐ 簧片归类：按簧管三分法（单簧 / 双簧 / 自由簧），巴乌与笙同列自由簧；「巴乌的簧片虽为硬质铜簧，由于做得很薄和舌端渐窄，振动受空气柱影响，兼具软簧性质。笙的铜簧也很薄，须与空气柱耦合振动。这两种乐器均为世所鲜见的特例」；「自体簧的簧舌是从座板或管体本身割出，如巴乌 · 中国笙」依乐器分类资料
