The software synthesizer

Oscillation, filtering and modulation computed in software, with results shaped by implementation and runtime conditions

2 min readElectronicUpdated 2026-11-02

In 1997 a computer screen held two TB-303s and a TR-808 side by side, panels drawn to match the originals, knobs you could turn with a mouse. The sound was computed on the spot, and none of the machines was in the room.

Three machines inside one computer

ReBirth RB-338 came from Propellerhead: two 303s and an 808, with the interface copied from the hardware. Computers and sound cards of the day could not synthesize in real time, so it played samples of the originals instead — a simulation built out of recordings. The step sequencer and pads underneath sidestepped the keyboard entirely, and someone who could not play could make a groove within minutes. The two authors used the proceeds to build Reason.

Ten milliseconds

In 1996 Steinberg's VST gave recording software real-time effect plugins. Propellerhead wanted ReBirth inside it and made ReWire; Steinberg took the hint and built VST Instruments, turning a software synth into a plugin. What actually made it playable was ASIO: in 1999, ASIO 2.0 pushed latency below ten milliseconds. No ear can hear the gap between key and sound at that distance, and the software had waited two years for exactly those ten milliseconds. The same year, Native Instruments released Pro-Five, a software Prophet-5.

A red case and modules on a screen

In 1995 Clavia's Nord Lead used DSP to model the oscillators and filters of old analog machines, in a red case with knobs across the panel. The digital era handed the knob back its dignity. Nord Modular went further in 1997 and moved the whole synthesis engine onto the screen: modules, cables, saved patches. Modular no longer needed a wall, or the fear of pulling the wrong cable.

Perfect digital, dearer analog

Compare software and hardware through synthesis method, polyphony, modulation routing, controller mapping and how patches are recalled with a project. A software preset can be restored with the session; hardware also requires its state and connections to be recorded. Judge a sound in the arrangement rather than treating digital as perfect and analog as inherently characterful, or assigning second-hand price changes to one technology.

A laptop now holds a whole studio. What decides who a record sounds like is still which knobs somebody turned, and how far.
