Yu (mouth organ)

The sheng grown large — most famous for the time someone did not play it

Related

PitchShengPaixiao (Chinese panpipes)DiziXun

The yu is the sheng grown large — the same principle, taken to its limit.

More reeds, a bigger gourd chest, four chi two cun long (nearly a metre). And its most famous appearance is precisely the time someone did not play it — lan yu chong shu: in King Xuan of Qi's band of three hundred, Master Nanguo faked it for a lifetime.

⚠️ But while it lived, its post was far above "faker" — the Han Feizi puts it plainly: "The yu is the eldest of the five tones; when the yu starts, bell and zither follow." ⭐⭐ Rule ㉜: "larger" was once a qualification, then became a burden — the yu rose with the big band and died as the band shrank. Warring States and Han could hold "three hundred at once"; by the Song the court's thirteen divisions had "only a sheng section, no yu section" — what retired it was not an enemy but its little brother, the sheng. And today one line of sheng reform is to build back up to thirty-six reeds: "large" was never wrong — it just came back under another name.

The sheng grown large

Three points:

1. One family, two sizes. The yu works exactly like the sheng (free reeds, reed-pipe coupling); the difference is scale — "the sheng is small with few reeds; the yu is large with many." The dividing line the ancients drew was reed count: "centre pipe, 36 reeds: yu; side pipe, 19 down to 13: sheng" (Book of Song). 2. How far was "to the limit"? Zheng Xuan's note to the Rites of Zhou: "the yu, 36 reeds; the sheng, 13"; Jia Gongyan's sub-commentary: "the yu is four chi two cun long" — by Han measure, nearly a metre of reed instrument. 3. ⭐ The object speaks. Mawangdui's yu (22 reeds) is not merely "more pipes": it has folded pipes and four twin finger-holes for the low register, with bronze reeds — two thousand years ago, this instrument was already solving the bass problem with pipe geometry.

"When the yu starts, bell and zither follow": it led

In its lifetime the yu was no "faker's instrument" — it was the lead (the eldest member of this batch's "who gives orders" family, rules ⑰–⑱):

  • "The yu is the eldest of the five tones; when the yu starts, bell and zither follow." (Han Feizi)
  • Scale was normal. King Xuan's "three hundred at once"; Linzi's citizens "all play the yu and pluck the se" — in the Warring States the yu was a civic-grade instrument.
  • It led the baixi spectacles: Western Han tomb figurines and Eastern Han stone reliefs show it holding an important place in those bands.
  • ⭐ And the "yu pitch-pipes" buried at Mawangdui show its relation to the standard: twelve pipes inscribed with the twelve lü names — ancient tuning pipes, the first such find in archaeology. Its very name carries "tuning" (⚠️ research suggests this set may have been a burial replica: its measurements do not match the Han standard). (The paixiao on this site also did the ancient band's tuning — both the gourd and bamboo families produced "measuring instruments.")

Rise and fall

The withdrawal left clear records:

PeriodState
Warring States–Hancivic bloom (Linzi) · court spectacle ("three hundred") · lead ("yu first, bell and zither follow") · star of baixi bands
Northern–Southern dynasties to Tang"the yu was used only in court ritual music"; Emperor Wenzong ruled it playable only in his "Cloud Shao" music; dropped from the nine and ten ensembles
Song"the yu vanished; of the court's thirteen divisions, only a sheng section, no yu section"; thereafter "the sheng replaced it"; in 1088 a 19-reed "yu-sheng" appears
todaysheng reforms include 21 · 24 · 36 reeds — the 36-reed "great sheng" is back, under the name sheng

⭐ Note the sharpest lesson in that curve — it was retired by its little brother:

  • yu and sheng differ only in scale (large-many vs small-few);
  • as ensembles moved from "three hundred in unison" toward finer, more varied scoring, larger meant dearer, heavier, less nimble;
  • what survived was the one that did the same job smaller.
  • And when modern sheng builders went large again (36 reeds), it was new materials and craft that made it viable — "large" was never the problem; the age that could carry "large" was.

"Lan yu chong shu": the most famous appearance

The Han Feizi tells it like a fable already polished:

"King Xuan of Qi had men play the yu — always three hundred at once. Master Nanguo asked to play, and the king was pleased, feeding him from the same stipend as hundreds. When the king died and King Min took the throne, who loved to hear them one by one — and the gentleman fled."

  • With three hundred blowing together, whether one man plays is inaudible — the cover of the ensemble;
  • heard one by one, he is exposed in a second — the mirror of the solo.
  • ⭐ So the instrument's strangest legacy: its most famous appearance is someone not playing it. The idiom "lan yu chong shu" (passing off fakes among the yu) has outlived the yu itself — rule ⑪ says a name can leave its instrument; the yu gives the blackest-comic version: the instrument is gone, and the story still teaches people not to fake it.
  • ⭐ And the ancients once rated its sound seriously: the Book of Rites — "when the gentleman hears the yu and sheng, he thinks of ministers who gather people together." An instrument later buried under a joke was first heard as "gathering."

Common misconceptions

  • "The yu is just another word for the sheng." ⚠️ One family, two instruments, divided by reed count — 22 and up is a yu, 19 and down a sheng.
  • "The yu died because it sounded bad." ⚠️ No such evidence: it withdrew through changes in scoring — from civic and baixi star to court-ritual-only to the Song court's "no yu section," replaced by the sheng of the same family.
  • "The idiom means the yu was worthless." ⚠️ The opposite: its premise is that three hundred playing together was a magnificent spectacle — the yu was "eldest of the five tones," able to mount such a scene. What was wrong was the man who snuck in.
  • "The yu exists only in books." ⚠️ There are objects: the Mawangdui yu (22 reeds, bronze reeds, folded pipes, twin holes) and a set of "yu pitch-pipes" inscribed with the twelve lü; Tang sheng and yu even travelled to Japan and survive in the Shōsōin.
  • "It vanished utterly." ⚠️ The name lived on to 1088 (the "yu-sheng"), and its "large" has revived in today's sheng (36 reeds) — just not under the name yu.

Timbre, and how to hear it

Three cues:

1. Hear the "family." Same principle as the sheng — the point is not a different colour but bulk: more reeds, longer pipes, a bigger chest. 2. The sound of "gathering." The Book of Rites heard "ministers who gather people" in it — the free reed is sustaining, dense, and dozens can stack without fighting — that is the acoustics behind "three hundred at once." 3. Hear its post. "When the yu starts, bell and zither follow" — listen imagining the lead: it sounds, and only then does the band move.
