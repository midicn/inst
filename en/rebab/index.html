⭐⭐ The main line: the one the violin replaced

「the ancestor of almost all European bowed instruments」——the site's most ironic tale of birth and ending

The rebab is the ancestor of the medieval European rebec (11th–18th centuries) and of the Byzantine lira: its pear-shaped variant reached Western Europe in the 11th century and became the rebec, and that rebec line is one of the ancestors of the violin family. ⭐⭐ In other words, a stretch of the violin's blood is rebab blood.

⭐⭐ And then it was pushed out in its own homeland — across the Arab world the rebab was gradually displaced by the violin and by the kemenche. It became the great-great-great-grandparent of its own successors, and the first member of its own family to be swept out of the house.

⭐⭐ And the sharper layer of it is in Turkey: in the second half of the 18th century viola d'amore arrived from Europe and was taken up enthusiastically, and Turkey's own bowed instrument, the kemence, fell out of favour in secular music, ⭐⭐ surviving only in the Mevlevi lodge under the name rebab. ⭐⭐ The old names kemân and kemence were forgotten, and rebab became its only name — the lodge even attributed a kind of sacredness to the kemence.

⭐⭐ A rule worth carrying away: when an instrument is displaced, what usually survives is not its shape but the position it occupied — ⭐⭐ the rebab lost its place in secular music to the violin and kept its place in the lodge; the lodge wanted a bowed string, not a violin. ⭐⭐ It gave up everything in the Arab world except a position, and a borrowed name (against the site's "instrument as position" line: the ney holds the slot of blowing, the oud holds the slot of maqam, the rebab holds the slot of the one bowed string in the lodge).

⭐⭐ An iron spike on the floor

⭐ Form: a small round or coconut-shell body · the front covered with sheepskin or parchment · a long thin neck ending in a pegbox · one to three strings (the Iraqi joza has four) · no fingerboard · an iron spike on the bottom that stands on the floor.

⭐⭐ Which is why it is a spike fiddle: not held at the shoulder, not tucked under the chin, but standing on the floor like a cello — and that spike is the whole manual for its posture. ⭐⭐ A whole set of trade-offs grows out of it: the body stands, close to the ground and the legs, and the skin face radiates directly, so few overtones, a hard attack, right next to the body; ⭐⭐ and the price is no ease of moving quickly and no room for high positions — ⭐⭐ which is both the physical reason for its voice-like tone and the physical reason the violin pushed it out of secular music.

⭐ Bow: far more curved than a violin bow, and the hair is usually horsehair; ⚠️ whether the hair sits between the two strings or outside them differs from one school to another — so this page records only the certain fact, that it is bowed. ⭐⭐ The full account of the bowed family is at bow technique and character: the Chinese bowed instruments split into nine with the hair between the strings and four with it outside, ⭐⭐ and the rebab stands right on that watershed — the morin khuur's habit of rubbing with the hair outside the two strings is one concrete coordinate on that line.

⭐⭐ Among this fretless, bowed family the rebab is the smallest: the oud sings maqam microtones off a fretless board, the yehu keeps its place as a quiet second part, ⭐⭐ and the rebab pushes the fretless idea to its limit — one string to three, with the pitch found by the finger stopping the string directly.

⭐⭐ A little over an octave: it wins on tone, not on pitch

⭐⭐ Reliable sources give its compass only as a qualitative description — a little over an octave — and record its virtue elsewhere: valued for its voice-like tone.

⭐⭐ This is the site's best bargain: it gives up compass to buy a throat that sits next to a human voice. ⭐⭐ Narrow is not a defect but a division of labour — with one to three strings and no fingerboard, the pitch is found by the finger stopping the string directly, and there are only so many places to stop it; ⭐⭐ rather than make the body bigger and the compass wider, it made its timbre look like a voice.

⭐⭐ Two things come out of this table: first, from the rebab to the violin, the compass buys the right to stand alone; second, ⭐⭐ the rebab is the only bowed instrument here that does not stand on its compass at all — it is closer to a reed beaten by a bow, a skin that talks.

⭐⭐ A map of names: everywhere it has another name

⭐⭐ The list of rebab aliases is longer than its list of features: the Arabic ربابة · joza / jawza in Iraq and Syria · rebab in Turkey · rabab in Egypt and the Mediterranean · ghichak / kabuli rabab in Afghanistan and Pakistan · kamanche / kamancha in Persia, meaning the small bow · kamanga in Kuwait and Egypt · the gamelan rebab in Indonesia · saw sam sai in Thailand.

⭐⭐ The name kamanche is itself a translation accident: the Persian kamancha means the small bow, and the Turkish kemânçe inherited the word — ⭐⭐ and the kemence was the only bowed instrument in Turkish music before the 18th century, its old body hollowed from a coconut and its face covered with sheatfish skin or ox pericardium, in two-string and three-string variants alike. ⭐⭐ A people named their only bowed instrument the small bow, and another people then took its name away from it — in the end even the name was borrowed.

⭐⭐ Names never come from the form — joza names the shell, kamanche names the bow, rebab says nothing at all; ⭐⭐ and the only one that states its origin is ghichak, a local name in the Afghan region. The name history of an instrument is usually the history of who took it over.

⭐⭐ The two fiddles of Java: one pelog, one slendro

⭐⭐ The rebab of the Indonesian gamelan is the strangest branch of all: only two strings · the body is a coconut shell covered with skin · the bow hair is horsehair, and ⭐⭐ the bow is never kept taut — the tension of the string is controlled by the bow player's own hand. ⭐⭐ This is not a flaw but a design: every stroke of the bow very slightly tunes the string, so the same note can carry different slight differences under different hands.

⭐⭐ The gamelan keeps two rebabs — one pelog, one slendro — ⭐⭐ and the two never play together. ⭐⭐ One instrument, one set of pitches split into two, ⭐⭐ and two that never meet is the strangest scoring on the site (against the site's "one instrument, two jobs": the erhu covers two parts with one · the gamelan rebab is one instrument holding two slots).

⭐⭐ The rebab also grew a second life in Indonesia as an instrument in singers' hands — after the singer and rebab player K.P.H. Notoprojo, that road, where the fiddle speaks in place of the voice, kept running through South-East Asia.

⭐⭐ A rule to take away: what survives is the position, not the shape

⭐⭐ Back to the most ironic thread: it is the ancestor of nearly every European bowed string, and it was the violin that put it out in its own home. ⭐⭐ A descendant turning its ancestor out of the door is extraordinarily rare in the history of instruments — and the rebab is one of the cases.

⭐⭐ Three judgements worth carrying away:

⭐⭐ One · after being displaced, what survives is the position. The rebab gave way to the violin in secular music and kept its place in the Mevlevi lodge — the lodge wanted a bowed string, not a violin. ⭐⭐ It lost everything it had in the Arab world except a place to stand.

⭐⭐ Two · when the position changes, the name has to change. The old names kemân and kemence were forgotten and rebab became its only name. ⭐⭐ That is not a renaming; it is the fact that from then on the thing was called that name in that one setting only — its name shrank to the single use that was left to it. ⭐⭐ When an instrument survives in a single setting, its name contracts into that setting too.

⭐⭐ Three · form is the easiest thing to sacrifice and the least worth defending. ⭐⭐ Not one of its spike, coconut shell or sheepskin was preserved as form when the violin displaced it — ⭐⭐ in the end the rebab lived on as a borrowed name plus a fixed occasion. ⭐⭐ So what it left behind was not a shape but a rule: after an instrument is displaced, what saves it is the position it holds, and whether it is willing to change its name for that position.
