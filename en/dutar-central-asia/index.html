The Central Asian dutar

A name that stops counting once it crosses a border — the two in dutar is a badge of origin, not a spec

2 min read613 characters条目World InstrumentsUpdated 2026-10-21

The name is a spec sheet. Persian du is two, tar is string: the instrument with two strings. Uzbek writes dutor, Turkmen and Afghan write dutar, Bengali writes dotara.

The name says two

Two strings: one walks the melody, one holds the floor. On the Central Asian side the left hand usually stops the first string with two fingers and the second with the thumb, and the second string often serves as a drone. The name states a quantity; playing turns it into a division of labour, and those are two different things.

Two, four, fourteen

The trouble begins when the name crosses a border. The Herat dutar of Afghanistan has fourteen strings; the Bengali dotara has four; modern Persian-language dutars also come in four-string forms, while most Uzbek, Tajik, Turkmen and Uyghur instruments still have two.

One name, three numbers, none of them connected. Each place altered the instrument to its own purposes, and the name records the shape it was most typical in. So the nature of the name changes: while it still fits, it is a specification; once it does not, it is a birth certificate.

One long-handled ladle

The form is shared: a long-handled pear-shaped body, often built up from glued strips of wood with a half-round ridge along the seam; a thin mulberry soundboard with no sound holes; a slender neck wound with sixteen to eighteen silk frets; a flat head with T-shaped pegs. One to two metres long, the largest reaching three.

Five peoples share the same box, the same neck, the same way of winding frets. Since the shell is common, divergence happens where it is cheapest: in the strings. Changing strings means changing neither box nor neck nor fingering.

Two hands, one frame drum

The right hand uses all five fingers, plucking, picking or sweeping; the left stops the first string with two fingers and the second with the thumb.

One point needs saying plainly. A source that has the Uyghur plucking and other peoples sweeping also lists the Uyghur among the sweepers, so ethnicity does not map onto technique.

The old partner on this side is the doira frame drum: the drum for rhythm, the dutar for melody and floor, both holding a singer up. Afghan solo and epic singing uses it too — one instrument keeping a long narrative company.

  • 对照线 ——dutar（维吾尔族都塔尔 · 站内另有一条）：同一个名字的两条分工——那一条讲「维吾尔族的都塔尔＝两条弦：第一弦主奏、第二弦持续音」与《十二木卡姆》、起源传说与乐器之母的地位；这一条讲中亚这一支的形制漂移（弦数从二到十四、琴体从长柄水瓢到更接近曼陀林）、多族共享一个名字而各自改造它、以及它与框鼓 doira 的搭档关系。tanbur（弹布尔）：同一片中亚—波斯音乐圈的同族长颈拨弦——但五弦分三组（两根外弦同度成组主奏 ＋ 三弦伴奏），都塔尔是两条；dombra（冬不拉）：哈萨克与卡拉卡尔帕克的两弦拨弦——琴身从长柄水瓢换成扁平或铲形、弦品从缠丝换成缠羊肠，同在中亚，同是两弦，一个被记住的是弦数，一个被记住的是民族；santur-persian（波斯桑图尔）：同为「乐器被某种体系约束」的标本——桑图尔被调式限制住（换调必须换琴），都塔尔被「二弦」这个名字限制住；cimbalom（钦巴龙）：同族的彻底工程化——加腿加踏板加到全半音，把桑图尔换调的麻烦一次解决掉；daf（达夫鼓）：中亚一侧的老搭档——doira 框鼓与都塔尔配对给歌手伴奏；rebab（拉巴卜）：同一片世界的弓弦；erhu（二胡）与 yehu（椰胡）：中国尖杆琴与蒙皮共鸣的一支；ney（奈伊笛）与 oud（乌德琴）：同一个中东—中亚音乐圈里的邻居。
