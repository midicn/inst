The kobyz

Horsehair is its string, its bow, and the thing it learns to call like

Horsehair is its string, its bow, and the thing it learns to sound like. The pegbox is often a horse's head.

One horse

A harp needs two materials, a violin three. The kobyz needs one: horsehair for the strings, horsehair for the bow, a horse carved at the top, hooves in the music.

The material is its name too. Kyrgyz kyl kyyak: kyl means horsehair. The dutar says two and has variants with fourteen strings; this one has named its own body from the first and never changed its mind.

The Kazakhs call the short titled piece a küi, and imitating hooves is what the genre asks for.

The posture a rod decides

The body is carved from one block, apricot in Kyrgyz work, sixty to seventy centimetres, belly goatskin. A handle runs through it and ends in a spike.

The player clamps it between the knees like a cello, the bow passing outside the string. Posture follows the rod: with a rod beneath, the instrument can only stand, and the player can only sit behind it.

The rebab is a spike fiddle too, but its belly is skin; this one is a cavity cut from wood.

No fingerboard, one line

There is no board. The string is squeezed against the handle with the nail, so the pitch slides. The violin's position is in its frets, the erhu's in its positions, the oud's where finger meets string; the kobyz's is the pressure between nail and handle.

Two strings, one melody and one drone. In a jyrau's epic the drone holds the voice, and the fiddle takes the melody when the speaking stops.

Sacred, rebuilt, inscribed

It belonged to shamans and healers; legend gave it power over evil, illness and death.

In the 1930s it was remade with four metal strings for national orchestras, close enough to a violin to play the hardest repertoire. The rebuild entered the hall; the two-stringed one stayed in the village.

In 2025 Uzbekistan inscribed it on the UNESCO list, 02329. The jyrau's hoarse voice is now explained as an imitation of the kobyz: horsehair wears hoarse, and the throat follows.

  • 对照线 ——rebab（拉巴卜）： 同是尖杆弓弦，一蒙皮一整木，一被小提琴淘汰一被体制改造——拉巴卜的共鸣箱是一整张羊皮或羊皮纸、1–3 根弦、在阿拉伯世界被小提琴逐步取代；库布兹是整块木头挖制、两根马鬃弦、在苏联时期被改造成四弦小提琴式的乐团乐器； 一个被市场淘汰，一个被体制改造——「消失」与「变形」是两种完全不同的结局。erhu（二胡）与 yehu（椰胡）： 中国尖杆琴族——同为杆贯穿琴身、蒙皮共鸣、竖持，但二胡有指板、库布兹没有；matouqin（马头琴）： 同一个「马头」在两件乐器上的意义完全不同——马头琴的马头是形制的标记，库布兹的马头是被模仿的对象；bowed-family（拉弦乐器弓法与韵味）： 中国拉弦族分「弓在弦内」与「弓在弦外」两派，库布兹的短弓绷马鬃是又一种配置。violin（小提琴）与 cello（大提琴）： 库布兹的「改造目标」与「大提琴的姿势」——1930 年代那把四弦金属弦的库布兹是照着小提琴改的，而它的原始姿势却像大提琴；dombra（冬不拉）与 dutar（都塔尔）： 哈萨克与中亚的拨弦邻居—— 同样是两弦，一边是拨弦一边是擦弦；santur-persian（波斯桑图尔）： 同为「乐器被某种体系约束」的标本—— 桑图尔被调式限制住（换调必须换琴），库布兹被乐团编制改造。
