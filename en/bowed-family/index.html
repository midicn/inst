Bow technique and character in the bowed strings

Thirteen bowed strings: where the bow bears, how many strings, where the sound comes from

Thirteen bowed strings side by side. Three questions separate them: where the bow bears, how many strings, where the colour comes from.

Inside the strings, or outside

Nine keep the hair between two strings: erhu, banhu, jinghu, gaohu, zhonghu, gehu, zhuihu, yehu and sihu. Four set it against them from outside: matouqin, aijieke, saer, niutuiqin.

The difference shows at the third question. Held by the strings, the bow bears both ways, one note at a time. Balanced alongside, it bears one way and touches two strings. The matouqin does it by nature; the others work for it — the sihu splits its bow hair, one strand per string.

The outside group shares one rule: hair and string stay at right angles, because the bow is not held by the strings but balanced beside them. That decides three things: how many notes, whether the hand manages the angle, which way the tone faces.

How many strings

One: the niutuiqin, an octave wide. Narrow is a strength — no rewriting tunes for a singer. Two is standard. Four: the sihu, 1 and 3, 2 and 4 in unison, so a new key needs no moved bridge.

Two other answers. The saer has one melody string and nine to thirteen unstopped strings tuned to the harmonic series, colouring only; the aijieke's resonance strings each have a pitch and carry melody. One is an engineer, the other a player.

Five places colour comes from

Where the skin is: the matouqin's double face seals the sound into a smaller space. A row of unstopped strings, rare outside this family. One adjustable part: the niutuiqin's post, turnable while playing. Where the bow is: outside means double stops. And the bluntest: borrow someone else's strings.

Change strings, or change fingering

Two routes to any key. More strings: the sihu's four, the matouqin's three tunings, the aijieke's three registers. Or a fuller fingerboard: the saer has eighteen frets plus five on the soundboard in equal temperament, one fixed melody string — any key without restringing, at the price of one octave.

Three times the family borrowed a finished answer: the bow outside, the low aijieke's cello strings and bow, the gehu laid sideways for the bass.

Bowing is not only pulling: long, short, linked, separate, stopped, struck, broken, trembling, springing and chopping strokes, the last few added to the matouqin in Qi Baoligao's third reform. Change the stroke, change the gesture, change the tone.
