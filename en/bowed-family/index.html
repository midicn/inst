Bow technique and character in the bowed strings

Thirteen bowed strings: where the bow bears, how many strings, where the sound comes from

Thirteen bowed strings are done in this section. What separates them comes down to three questions:

Where does the bow bear? How many strings? Where does the colour come from?

⭐⭐ This page adds no new instrument — it simply lays the thirteen side by side, and only then do three dividing lines appear.

⭐⭐ Dividing line 1: bow inside the strings, or outside

The hardest gate in this family. Of the thirteen, nine have the hair between the strings, four graze outside them:

Bow between (nine)⭐ Bow outside (four)
Whoerhu · banhu · jinghu · gaohu · zhonghu · gehu · zhuihu · yehu · sihu⭐ matouqin · aijieke · saer · niutuiqin
The bow's situation⭐ held by the strings, bears on both sides⭐ set against them from outside, bears one way only
⭐⭐ How many pitches at onceone at a time within a position⭐⭐ two at once (an interval)
The hand's anglethe bow is supported, the angle is forgiving⭐⭐ you manage it yourself — 「keep the hair and the string at right angles」
Which way the tone facessoft · clear · inward⭐ broad · low · free

⭐⭐ Look at the third row: moving the bow outside buys a capability nothing else has.

The matouqin did it first — and to get double stops the others must work: ⭐ the sihu splits its bow hair in two; ⭐ the erhu presses the hair against both strings. ⭐⭐ The matouqin does nothing special — it can do it by nature.

⭐⭐ "Keep the right angle" is the common requirement of the outside-bow group, because the hair is not held by the strings but set against them from outside — so the player owns the angle.

⭐⭐ Dividing line 2: how many strings

The answers run from one to one plus a row, and each answer buys something different:

StringsWho⭐⭐ What it buys
one (about an octave)⭐ niutuiqin⭐ narrow is a strength: no need to rewrite tunes for a singer
twoerhu · banhu · jinghu · gaohu · zhonghu · gehu · zhuihu · yehu · aijieke (dolan) · saer · matouqinthe standard
⭐ two, but only one is bowed⭐⭐ saer（1 melody + 9–13 resonance）⭐ the resonance strings only add colour
four⭐ sihu（1·3 and 2·4 in unison）· the modern aijieke⭐ new tuning without moving the bridge
⭐ a row that is never stopped⭐⭐ aijieke（5–10）· saer（9–13）see below
several tunings / registers⭐ matouqin（three tunings）· aijieke（high / middle a fifth lower / low an octave lower）⭐ pitch relationships set by restringing

⭐⭐ Dividing line 3: where the colour comes from

There are five completely different methods, and each family's own "signature" sits in one of the cells:

① Where the skin is ⭐⭐ Skin on both faces（matouqin）→ the sound is sealed in a smaller space → thick and dark ② A row of un-stopped resonance strings ⭐⭐ the only family anywhere that plays this game (see below) ③ One adjustable part ⭐⭐ the sound post（niutuiqin）→ ⭐⭐ the only instrument here adjustable while playing ④ Where the bow is ⭐⭐ outside the strings → two pitches at once（matouqin） ⑤ Just borrow someone else's strings ⭐ the low aijieke's strings are cello strings

⭐⭐ None of the five is better — they just answer different questions. Want double stops? move the bow. Want double stops from an in-between bow? handcraft the hair. Want to sit against a voice? add something you can turn with one hand.

⭐⭐ Side line 1 · a row of un-stopped strings: three divisions of labour

Only this family carries a row of strings that the left hand never stops — they sound only because the melody strings set them going. ⭐⭐ But three methods exist, and they produce entirely different results:

⭐ MethodWho⭐⭐ What it produces
⭐ split the bow hair in twosihu⭐⭐ one strand per string → two unison strings speak → two pitches
⭐ each resonance string tuned to its own pitch⭐⭐ aijieke⭐⭐ g · c¹ · d¹ · e¹ · g¹ · a¹ · b¹ · d² → ⭐⭐ it carries the melody — it is a player
⭐ resonance strings tuned to the harmonic series⭐⭐ saer⭐⭐ only resonance and colour → ⭐⭐ it is a sound engineer

⭐⭐ Within one family: one makes the bow do handwork, one makes the strings sing, one makes the strings colour the tone.

⭐⭐ And none of the four is the first inventor — the sihu, aijieke, saer, rawap and seyit fall into different traditions (northeastern Chinese, Central Asian, 回部乐). ⭐⭐ They share the idea, each with its own solution.

⭐⭐ Side line 2 · two ways to modulate

There are only two routes to "can play in any key":

Route A · more strings, so you can change them ⭐ sihu（four strings）· matouqin（three tunings: g·c¹ / d¹·a / d¹·g） · ⭐ aijieke（three registers）

Route B · few strings, but a complete fingerboard ⭐⭐ saer: 18 frets on the neck plus 5 high frets on the soundboard, ⭐ laid out in twelve-tone temperament → ⭐⭐ with one fixed melody string it can play in any key without restringing or moving the bridge

⭐⭐ These are the two ideas worth learning from this whole family: change the strings, or change the fingering. Most of the first nine went the "change the strings" way; ⭐ the saer proves "few strings" can work too — at the price of one octave of range.

⭐⭐ Side line 3 · borrowing someone else's finished solution

Three places in this family, meeting a problem they could not solve, chose to borrow one that was already solved:

⭐ matouqin · moved the bow outside the strings → ⭐⭐ double stops for free, while the others must work for them

⭐ the low aijieke · borrowed the cello outright 1960s, Urumqi instrument factory: ⭐⭐「采用大提琴弦，使用大提琴弓……⭐ 其演奏姿势和方法均与大提琴相同」

⭐ gehu · lay horizontal to supply the bass

⭐⭐ One judgement shared by all three: rather than invent a flawed new solution, import a finished one.

⭐ The bow techniques: bowing is not just "pulling"

⭐ Right hand: 长弓 · 短弓 · 连弓 · 分弓 · 顿弓 · 击弓 · 碎弓 · 抖弓 · 跳弓 · 连跳弓 · 砍弓 · 连砍弓

⭐⭐ The last few were only added to the matouqin in Qi Baoligao's third reform, together with techniques from the violin, erhu, sihu and cello (see Matouqin).

⭐⭐ The names themselves carry the action: ⭐ 跳弓 breaks the string into points · ⭐ 碎弓 rapid alternation · ⭐ 砍弓 chops. ⭐ Change the bow technique and you change the action; change the action and you change the tone.

⭐ Left hand: 揉 · 滑 · 颤 · 带 · 打音 · 擞音 · 泛音 · 拨弦 · 弹弦 ⭐⭐ Every instrument here has 揉, and ⭐⭐ 揉 is the one gesture most like the human voice — which is why the saer and the niutuiqin both lean on it to sit close to a sung line.

⭐⭐ Recap: three sentences to remember this family

Nothing in this family is the standard answer. ⭐ Want double stops? move the bow（matouqin）. Want double stops without moving it? handcraft the hair（sihu）. Want to sit against a voice? add a part you can turn（niutuiqin）. Need a bass? don't build one — borrow a set（the low aijieke）.
