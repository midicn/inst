A cappella singing

No instruments — voices alone become the whole band; "a cappella" was born of a misunderstanding

Related

VoiceChoir scoringBel cantoTimbre

⭐⭐ The main line: a term went viral not through understanding but through misunderstanding

「The term a cappella was originally intended to differentiate between Renaissance polyphonic and Baroque concertato musical styles. In the 19th century, a renewed interest in Renaissance polyphony, coupled with an ignorance of the fact that vocal parts were often doubled by instrumentalists, led to the term coming to mean unaccompanied vocal music.」

⭐⭐ A new extreme in the site's name-vs-reality lineage — bel canto was a term that arrived thirty years after the singing died; a cappella runs the other way: the meaning "unaccompanied" was produced by a 19th-century misunderstanding.

The truth — Renaissance polyphony's parts were often doubled by instruments ("the early a cappella polyphonies were sometimes doubled with other instruments"), and even the supposed a cappella stronghold shows evidence: "some of the early pieces by Palestrina, such as those written for the Sistine Chapel, were intended to be accompanied by an organ doubling for some or all of the voices." 19th-century revivalists did not know this, and mistook a style (Renaissance polyphony, as against Baroque concertato) for a configuration (no instruments) — after which the whole world sang that misreading for a hundred and fifty years.

⭐⭐ Compare the site at large — talk box's three claimants fought over "who"; a cappella's misunderstanding was over "what" — and the misunderstanding won. The history of a term is written not by those who understand it but by those who misread it, because the misreaders are many.

⭐⭐ The other half: voices carrying three jobs alone

Misunderstanding aside, what the misunderstanding built is genuinely moving: without instruments, voices must carry melody, harmony and rhythm themselves.

⭐ Melody to the lead vocalist, harmony to the supporting voices, rhythm to vocal percussion — "rhythm (achieved through vocal percussion or rhythmic phrasing)". ⭐⭐ A choir is a self-sufficient band (compare choir-scoring's family of scorings — there parts built harmonic density; here parts cover the whole division of labour in a band).

⭐⭐ With no instrument as pitch reference, intonation must be built into every singer — the hardest gate: instruments are pitch's plug-in; voices must build it in. Barbershop's close harmony "involves tightly spaced intervals that produce resonant overtones" — dense spacing forces ringing overtones, verifying pitch with the physics of the ear.

⭐ Byzantine rite: the strictest a cappella came from reading one verse

Orthodox liturgical music is entirely unaccompanied: "In the Orthodox Church today, as in the early Church, singing is unaccompanied and instrumental music is not found." (Bishop Kallistos Ware) — rooted in a strict reading of Psalm 150: "Let every thing that hath breath praise the Lord."

⭐⭐ "Let every thing that hath breath praise the Lord" — what has breath is the throat — this reading turned "having breath" into "only using breath." One doctrinal reading produced a thousand-year unaccompanied tradition (compare the church's ban on female singing that produced the castrati — two prohibitions, two consequences: one banned instruments, one banned women).

⭐⭐ Each step of secularisation was a subtraction of equipment: first no instruments (spirituals — the reality of enslaved communities), then no band scoring (quartets), then no conductor, no score (college groups) — the modern history of a cappella is the band's accessories being removed one by one; and with each removal, the voice grew one more capability.

⭐ Beatboxing is the terminal station of this line: "vocal percussion, or beatboxing, uses the mouth to create drum-like sounds" — ⭐⭐ compare vocal-production: the vocal folds are wind-blown reeds; beatbox turns lips, tongue and throat into percussion simultaneously — one instrument becomes a whole rhythm section (the human groovebox — compare the groovebox entry's "one person is the band"; here it is "one throat is the band").

⭐⭐ The Chinese line: the valley is older than the chapel

A cappella did not begin in the chapel — it began in the valley — the voice is the factory setting; instruments are the add-ons (echoing the voice entry's "singing predates spoken language").

⭐ Call-and-response work songs, mountain-song antiphony between two groups, a mother's lullaby — all older than the Sistine's polyphony. ⭐ And Dong grand song (no conductor, no instruments, polyphony — recorded in choir-scoring) gives a pointed contrast: scoring is a Western invention; polyphony is humanity's common property — the West took five centuries to turn "a cappella" from misunderstanding into discipline; singers in the valleys never heard the word and never needed instruments.

⭐⭐ Closing: two extremes

Compare Telharmonium — it wanted to put the whole band onto wires (a 200-ton machine pursuing "no players"); a cappella unplugs the wires and returns 200 tons to zero. ⭐⭐ The head and tail of the site's electronic music history: one "all electrified", one "all embodied" — yet both pursued the same thing: music that depends on no particular device.
