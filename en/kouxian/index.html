The wind section ends with its one "boundary case."

The jaw harp makes no sound from airflow — it is a plucked idiophone: a fixed-at-one-end lamella vibrates, and the mouth amplifies and shapes it. Small enough for a pocket (8–9 cm), yet called "the first instrument humanity possessed" — "humanity's first sound."

⚠️ And its most astonishing fact comes from Shimao, Shaanxi, in 2017–2018: twenty-odd bone jaw harps from the Huangchengtai, about 4,000 years old — the earliest and largest find of its kind anywhere in the world. The report's conclusion: the Hetao region of north China may be the "homeland" of the world's jaw harps. ⭐⭐ Rule ㊴: an eight-centimetre object that moved the coordinates of world music history — "humanity's first sound" and a diffusion line from north China to the Eurasian steppe. It is also why the wind section ends here: the last lesson is where "wind instrument" ends.

How it sounds: not on a string, in the mouth

Three points:

1. A strip and a frame. "A thin lamella, fixed at one end, vibrates" — the Shimao pieces are frame-and-tongue in one, the tongue cut out of the bone itself. 2. The hand drives it, not the breath. Finger-plucking (or string-pulling) sets the tongue moving — hence plucked idiophone. The "string" is plucked by your finger and drawn by your mouth. 3. ⭐ Pitch and melody are separated: the reed gives a base vibration, the melody is selected as overtones by the mouth and tongue — your mouth is the soundbox.

⭐ And the report's account of playing is nearly a painting: "one hand pinches the foot, holds the harp loosely to the lips, the other pulls the cord… with mouth resonance and tongue position" — giving "chirring, humming" tones. The researcher imagines the empty terrace 4,000 years ago: "a sound like cicadas and swarming bees."

A line from north China to the Eurasian steppe

  • It still sounds worldwide: Inuit, Native American, Sámi, Ainu… an eight-centimetre object that is genuinely a world instrument.
  • ⭐ And archaeology has drawn its diffusion line: from about 2000 BCE to the 5th century CE — some 2,500 years — harps unearthed from north China, the Mongolian plateau and southern Siberia share the same structure, narrow strips, mostly string-pulled; "a diffusion line from north China toward the Eurasian steppe emerges naturally."
  • At home it lives among 30+ peoples under 50-plus names: Evenki "mukulian", Mongol "ahu min'er", Manchu "mokuni", Yi "honghong / nongguo", Dai "bai", Li "kougong"…
  • ⭐ Its uses have stayed the same: feelings, courtship, ritual, amusement, social life. In 2008 (Butuo Yi) and 2011 (Beichuan Qiang) it entered the national intangible-heritage list.

The name was wrong twice

Its commonest modern name is 口弦 — "mouth *string*" — and it has no string at all. The first mistake: early writers understood it by analogy with the zither's strings, and gave it a "string" character.

Its old name, "簧", was mistaken too: Han-dynasty *Shuowen* glosses it as "簧: the reed of a sheng," making it a part of a mouth organ. The archaeological view runs the other way: the pre-Qin "huang" was made of bone or bamboo, "sounded by pulling a cord — a separate instrument, not a sheng part."

⭐ Hence names-history item ⑱: a name built on a misunderstanding — "mouth string" mistook a reed for a string; "sheng's reed" mistook an instrument for a component. And when "huang" died out in the Central Plains after the Yuan, the wrong name was forgotten with it; from the Ming people simply called it "口琴" (mouth organ) — until modern archaeology and ethnography recognised it again: "口簧" is its true name.

Common misconceptions

  • "It is a wind instrument." ⚠️ It is a plucked idiophone: a lamella vibrates; airflow only *shapes*. This page keeps it in the wind section as the boundary case.
  • "It has a string." ⚠️ The name is wrong: no string — only a vibrating tongue. "Reed" (簧) is the accurate word.
  • "Just a small toy." ⚠️ Those 23 pieces came from the Shimao palace terrace; the pre-Qin "huang" was a noble's instrument, and a Han palace once held a "Reed Drumming Hall." It is the earliest jaw harp find in the world.
  • "It belongs only to minority peoples." ⚠️ It once flourished in the Central Plains — by the late 4th century it was "very popular" in Sichuan, Yunnan and Guizhou; it vanished there only after the Yuan. Today it lives among minorities *and* worldwide.
  • "You just blow." ⚠️ Its sound is held in the mouth: lips loose, cavity shaped, tongue moved — the same reed, a different world for every player.

Timbre, and how to hear it

Three cues:

1. Hear the tune inside the hum. The buzz is the reed's base tone; the melody is overtones picked by the mouth — so it sounds "out of tune" and yet in tune every phrase. 2. Hear "cicadas and bees." The Shimao researcher's phrase for a sound 4,000 years old. 3. Hear the smallness. A pocket instrument — and one of humanity's earliest instrument forms. Music need not be big.
