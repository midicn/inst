Yangqin (hammered dulcimer)

Two edits to become Chinese — first the hammer, then the name

Two bamboo hammers are held like chopsticks. The stroke is hard; the string keeps ringing after the hand lifts. The yangqin is struck, yet it trails a tail.

Two notes on one string

Two bridges stand on the soundboard like ridge lines: the peaks lift the strings, the valleys let the other bridge's strings pass through. Interleaved, they turn the board into a map of positions, treble bridge at the left, bass at the right.

Early instruments saved strings by a trick: a bridge at two-thirds of the length divides one string into two segments a fifth apart. One string, two notes.

Struck with bamboo, the sound is a point first and a line second. That is the part hardest to describe.

Change the tool, then the name

In 1744 two officials posted to Macao described a local instrument with copper strings, struck with split bamboo. Replacing the wooden hammer with bamboo was the first change: bamboo flexes, which allows rolls, sweeps and tremolo that a wooden head cannot.

The second change was the name. Qiu Heshou, in a handbook of qin studies, replaced the character for ocean, the usual prefix on imported goods, with one read the same way. The pronunciation stayed, the character moved.

The tool obeyed the hand; the name obeyed the ear.

Out of tune, then out of the octave

The old forms were narrow: the double-eight model ran f1 to c2, double-ten to d3, double-twelve to e3, an octave and a half that drifted. Reform came in steps. The 401 layout offered 54 positions from G to a³ with semitone adjusters for free transposition; the 402 lowered the bridges, added three semitone positions and switched to nickel-wound strings; the 501 of the late 1970s used five rows and 77 positions, E to c⁴, playable in all twelve keys.

The job is in the story-singing halls

Its busiest work is accompaniment, in narrative song and regional opera. The genre known as qinshu, literally qin books, is named after it: Shandong qinshu, Sichuan qinshu, Henan qinshu. It also sits in Cantonese music, Jiangnan sizhu, Changde strings and Yulin tunes.

Grain and sustain are opposites, and it manages both. Later models added a damper, because a long tail muddies the texture; knowing when to stop counts as skill.

Listen to a Shandong qinshu passage and catch the half-beat where the singer breathes. The yangqin fills it.

  • 来源与传入：「扬琴起源于古代两河流域的亚述地区，15 世纪经波斯传入欧洲并演变为德西马等变体。约明万历八年（1580 年）通过海上丝绸之路传入中国广东，初称桑图尔琴」；「（另有）中国本土学说……认为扬琴起源于扬州」；「现代学者认为扬琴是由其他国家传入的，有两条线路：一条……经海路从广东 · 中国中国澳门一带传入；另一条……走帕米尔高原地区的丝绸之路传入中国新疆地区」；「当下人们普遍认为扬琴是从其他国家传入中国的」；「扬琴是中国乐器中唯一的世界性乐器」；「最早的图像是苏美尔人……（公元前 4—3 世纪）使用扬琴的图像，保存在一个古老的苏美尔纪念碑上」依器物资料与百科资料
  • 形制：「琴身呈梯形，使用竹制琴槌击奏金属琴弦，音色清脆明亮，具有颗粒感和延绵感」；「传统扬琴……音箱呈蝴蝶形或扁梯形，长 90—97 · 宽 32—41 · 高 5.7—7 厘米……上置两个用竹 · 红木或牛角制 · 呈峰谷状条形琴码，左为高音码，右为低音码，峰部用以支弦，谷部有另一码上的琴弦通过」；「琴竹又称琴箭 · 琴签，为两支有弹性的竹制小棰」；「少者 8—10 档，多者 13—18 档，每档 2—4 根弦」；「初期的扬琴是梯形或长方形的琴箱，面板上张以几十条钢弦，在弦的 2/3 处支有条马，使每条弦发出五度关系的两个音」依百科与器物资料
  • 传统规格与音域：「常用扬琴有八音（实发二十四音）· 十音（实发三十音）· 十二音（实发三十六音）三种，又称双八型 · 双十型 · 双十二型。音域分别为：八音 f¹—c²，十音 d¹—d³，十二音 c—e³」；「传统扬琴有『双七音』『双八音』两种，音域仅一个半八度，并存在走音现象」依乐器资料
  • 用途（它最忙的地方）：「最初扬琴大多用于说唱音乐和地方戏曲伴奏。其中凡各地的『琴书』，都以扬琴为主要伴奏乐器」；「清末以来，扬琴用于广东音乐 · 江南丝竹 · 扬州清曲 · 广西文场 · 常德丝弦 · 四川琴书 · 河南琴书 · 山东琴书 · 榆林小曲等乐种 · 曲种」；「作为伴奏乐器，长期服务于琴书 · 戏曲及民族管弦乐」依器物资料
