Electronic drums

The mesh on a trampoline — a designer found the future of drumheads in a DIY store

A trampoline in the shop

The idea came from a DIY and garden store. A Roland mechanical designer named Yoshino was hunting for a pad surface with something like a drumhead's feel, and the films they had tried did not work. In the shop he saw a small trampoline whose bouncing surface was mesh. He came back with the proposal: use mesh for the pads.

Mesh was not easy to source. No Japanese supplier could make it well, so Roland's founder, Kakehashi, introduced the team to Remo Belli, the founder of Remo. Belli took the job. What they settled on was two plies of mesh, the weave set at 45 degrees, and they patented it.

Acoustic feel without the acoustic noise

Rubber pads felt more like a synth keyboard, and drummers did not accept them. Roland's brief became one sentence: the feel of an acoustic drum without its noise.

Rim shots were the sticking point. Playing a prototype upside down one day, they noticed that rubber poured into the bottom of the shell felt good and stayed quiet. They asked Remo to pour rubber on the top hoop too, and Remo made a custom mould. A whole kit could then be played with the sound in headphones and almost nothing in the room.

From Syndrum to V-Drums

The first electronic drum on the market was the Syndrum, in 1, Twin and Quad versions with one, two and four triggers. Dave Simmons founded his company in 1978 with hexagonal pads, and the biggest names of the eighties used them: Duran Duran, Def Leppard, Prince, Phil Collins, Rush, Depeche Mode.

Roland's Octapad of 1985 was the first percussion controller built to trigger samples and any MIDI device. In 1997 the TD-10 put mesh heads, a sound module and COSM modelling, borrowed from V-Guitar, into one kit; the 2012 TD-30KV moved to SuperNATURAL and behaviour modelling.

A kit is an ecosystem

The parts arrived one at a time: a mesh kick trigger in 1998, cymbals that sense bow and edge separately in 2001, a floating hi-hat in 2004, a home all-in-one in 2007, a marching unit in 2008, a drummer's social app in 2011.

Roland described the aim as the electric guitar for drummers: quiet practice, unlimited sounds, no tuning, one MIDI cable for recording. Forty years of that work made it the largest drum company in the world.
