The previous page (zhonghu) recorded something worth keeping: in one ensemble, the middle slot got filled and the bass slot still hasn't.

This page looks at one of the things people tried for that bass — the gehu.

And its method is the most interesting part:

"In the 1950s, Yang Yusen, after years of reform and practice, keeping the basic timbre and shape of the huqin family while incorporating the sounding structure of Western bowed-string instruments, produced it."

⭐⭐ Rule 53: a Chinese body with a Western voice — an instrument can keep the Chinese shape and take a Western throat.

Kept: the huqin's appearance (round box, python skin) and its colour ("tough and ringing", "its colour changes much as the erhu's does"). Swapped: all three of the sounding mechanisms follow the violin —

So put it this way: it looks like a huqin and sounds with a violin's throat.

It is one answer to the "missing bass" project

⭐ The background was given on the zhonghu page; the gehu's slot:

"Since the 1950s the low bowed strings developed include the dahu and dihu; the gehu and bass gehu; the big morin khuur; the laruan and so on. ⚠️ Because these reformed national bass instruments still have deficiencies in performance, most national orchestras now use the Western cello and double bass."

The gehu is the most hybrid of these answers — and the most interesting:

  • Dahu, dihu — more like an erhu made bigger and lower;
  • Laruan — another road (plucked, on a ruan-like body);
  • Big morin khuur — another people's bass;
  • ⭐ Gehu — keeping the huqin's shape and colour, replacing the sounding mechanism with the violin's.

And the outcome stands as above: it enriched the orchestra's bass range, yet did not displace the cello. Between "usable" and "standard in the ensemble" lies a gap called "deficiency".

Its honest weakness

⚠️ "Because the gehu's finger spacing is wide, it is not suited to very fast notes or a great deal of ornamentation."

— the same kind of candour as the zhonghu's "comparatively low agility": the bigger the instrument and the longer the strings, the coarser the fingers — and coarse fingers ornament slowly. Not a defect but the physical fate of large instruments.

⭐ It has its own compensation:

"Because the gehu's box is large, its strings long and its resonance long, players also pluck it from time to time."

— long strings → long ring → it can be "plucked" into thickness. An instrument designed for the bow taught itself the pluck.

Range

⚠️ Honest note: the sources give the width of the range (about three octaves) but no standard tuning or written pitch — so this figure is a schematic, unlike this site's measurement-based range figures, and the page says so.

Common misconceptions

  • "The gehu is just a cello." ⚠️ The shape and colour are the huqin's ("its colour changes much as the erhu's", "tough and ringing"); only the three sounding mechanisms were changed (horizontal, bow outside the strings, lever bridge). In range it covers the middle-low part, where the cello is the orchestral norm.
  • "China already had its own double bass." ⚠️ Many were tried (dahu, dihu, gehu, laruan, big morin khuur…) but "they still have deficiencies" → "most orchestras use the Western cello and double bass."
  • "It can play anything." ⚠️ "Not suited to very fast notes or much ornamentation" — a physical fate of size.
  • "Gehu and bass gehu are the same." ⚠️ Same outline, different size: the bass gehu is larger, "requires standing or sitting on a high stool", sounds "deep and full", and "its bowing follows the double bass."
  • "It is obsolete." ⚠️ Still in use — the Hong Kong, China Chinese Orchestra uses a single-skin gehu; the sources record its enriching of the orchestra's bass in positive terms.

Timbre, and how to hear it

Three cues:

1. Hear the weight. "Tough and ringing," and its ensemble job is "to thicken the sonority and enrich the harmony" — it is the layer that props the band up. 2. Hear the slow ornament. Wide finger spacing means its left hand does little — don't wait for it to run. 3. Hear it plucked sometimes. Long resonance invites players to pluck — a bowed instrument that sometimes sounds picked.

  • ⭐⭐ 创制（留形换嗓）：「革胡是胡琴类的低音拉弦乐器，二十世纪五十年代杨雨森经过多年的改革和实践后，在保留胡琴类乐器基本音色和造形的基础上，结合西方提琴类乐器的发音结构研制而成」；「革胡初期有小 · 中 · 大及低音革胡四种……大大丰富了民族乐队的低音域」依百科与辞书资料
  • ⭐⭐ 形制（三处"提琴化"）：「革胡由圆形和横置的木制琴筒组成，一端蒙有蟒蛇皮（单皮震动式或双皮震动式），用杠杆式琴码架起弦线，张弦四根」；「中国香港中乐团现时采用的革胡是单皮震动式革胡」依百科资料
  • ⭐⭐ 发声方式（与胡琴族不同）：「革胡以琴弓在弦外拉奏发声，或用右手手指拨弦弹奏。它的音色变化大致与二胡相同」；「由于革胡的琴箱较大 · 琴弦较长 · 馀音亦较长，因此亦不时会以拨弦的方式演奏」依百科资料
  • ⭐⭐ 音色 · 音域 · 声部：「革胡的音色坚韧响亮，音域宽广，约有三个八度」；「多负责演奏乐曲中的中低音声部，可演奏各种双音 · 和弦，而且适应任何转调」；「在合奏时，有增强音响厚度 · 丰富和声效果的作用」依百科资料
  • ⭐ 演奏技巧与短板：「右手主要是弓法技巧，有慢弓 · 快弓 · 分弓 · 连弓 · 断弓 · 顿弓 · 跳弓 · 抖弓等」；「至于左手的技巧则主要有泛音 · 颤音 · 打音和滑音等」；⚠️「由于革胡的指位距离比较宽，故此，它不适宜演奏过快的音符或者是过多的加花演奏」依百科资料
  • ⭐ 低音革胡（更矮一档）：「低音革胡的外形与革胡相同，但体形较大，同样是杨雨森于二十世纪五十年代在二胡基础上创制而成的低音拉弦乐器」；「由于它的体形较大，拉奏的时候需要站或坐在较高的椅子上。它的声音低沉浑厚，演奏方法与革胡一样，弓法亦如西洋乐器中的低音大提琴」依辞书资料
