Yue

One character, four identities — instrument, measure, bellows, key

Related

TimbrePaixiao (Chinese panpipes)ChiDiziXunGuanzi

This entry may be less about an instrument than about a character.

"龠" (yuè) has three identities in the dictionary and one more hidden in the oracle bones:

⚠️ Three in the dictionary: "a bamboo pipe of music" (instrument); "the measures — yue, ge, sheng, dou, hu" (a measure, its smallest unit); "between heaven and earth, is it not like the tuoyue?" (a bellows pipe, from the *Laozi*). Add the *Book of Documents*' "opened the yue and read the book" — it has also been a key. ⭐⭐ Rule ㊲: one hollow bamboo tube grew four meanings — and its oracle-bone glyph drew its shape earlier than any text did. What the four share is hollowness: the thing you blow, the thing you measure grain with, the thing that fans a fire, the thing that opens a lock. And more to the point: its first "record" is not a text but a glyph — the oracle-bone 龠 pictures bound tubes.

One character, four identities

IdentitySourceLine
1 · Instrument*Shuowen*"a bamboo pipe of music, three holes, to bring all voices into accord"
2 · Measure*Hanshu · Lülizhi*"the measures: yue, ge, sheng, dou, hu… two yue make a ge" — its smallest unit
3 · Bellows pipe*Laozi*"between heaven and earth, is it not like a tuoyue?"

⭐ Why one character? Because of hollowness: "since the bamboo yue is hollow, it was extended to mean the ancient measure" (source wording). To blow, to measure, to fan, to unlock — all four need only one empty tube.

The glyph file: the oracle bone sides with "bound tubes"

⭐ What makes this page unusual: the evidence is neither object nor text, but a glyph.

  • Oracle bones: "pictures bound tubes" (Guo Moruo: "showing this is *bound tubes*, not bound slips");
  • Shuowen: a three-hole pipe; Han notes say three, Mao and Zheng say six, *Guangya* says seven;
  • Modern scholars read the glyph and revive "bound tubes," "possibly the panpipes' forebear";
  • And the decisive sentence: "there is still no clear proof from excavated objects — therefore nothing is settled."

⭐ One possible peace: "there are a blowing yue and a dancing yue — the blowing one short with three holes, the dancing one long with six, to be held while dancing." Perhaps the contradicting hole counts were never the same object's.

The yue dance: the instrument of the "civil dance"

In Zhou ritual it was also a dance prop:

Zheng's commentary"the yue dance is the civil music"against the martial "wan dance"

⭐ An instrument of the "civil" side: the martial dance held shields and axes; the civil dance held this in the left hand and a plume in the right — the Zhou told "rite" from "war" by what you held.

The Huangzhong yue: one tube that measured China

Its deepest afterlife: it did not only sound — it measured grain.

  • *Hanshu · Lülizhi*: "the measures: yue, ge, sheng, dou, hu… all beginning from the Huangzhong yue … two yue make a ge, ten ge a sheng, ten sheng a dou, ten dou a hu";
  • And how much is one yue? "The yue is the substance of the Huangzhong pitch pipe… holding 1,200 grains of millet" — fill the Huangzhong pipe with 1,200 millet grains and you have one yue;
  • The *Zihui* is blunter: "all bamboo pipes of music are called yue, but the Huangzhong pipe, filled with millet, accumulates into the five measures' names."
  • Later granaries remembered: Li Shangyin's *Taicang Zhen* asks why the measures are cast in bronze — "because through cold and heat they do not change their capacity."

⭐ This links the site's older threads: the paixiao "checked the tuning" of the ancient band; the yu was buried with "yu pitch-pipes" — and the yue completes the line: Chinese capacity units were "measured" out of one pitch pipe. Music was once the standard itself.

Common misconceptions

  • "The yue is simply a three-hole flute." ⚠️ Three readings coexist: three-hole pipe / six-hole dance pipe / bound tubes, plus a seven-hole record — "no clear proof from excavated objects; nothing settled."
  • "An oracle-bone glyph says nothing about a real object." ⚠️ Here the glyph *is* the earliest record of the object: Guo Moruo reasoned from "bound tubes" to charge the *Shuowen* with "greatly betraying the ancient meaning."
  • "A character borrowed as a measure is coincidence." ⚠️ Not at all: "since the bamboo yue is hollow, it was extended to the ancient measure" — all four meanings share one physical trait.
  • "It survives only in books." ⚠️ It survives in units: "yue" entered the *Hanshu*'s system, measuring from "two yue to a ge" up to "ten dou to a hu."
  • "Civil and martial dances are just dancing." ⚠️ The *Rites of Zhou* created a yue-master's office; and "the wan enters, the yue is withdrawn" — switching dances switched instruments.

Timbre, and how to hear it

Three cues:

1. Hear "few." On the three-hole reading it could sound very few notes — Zhou ritual never asked it to show off; it asked it to "bring voices into accord." 2. Hear "accord." The *Shuowen*'s own definition is "three holes, to bring all voices into accord" — its post was blending, not leading. 3. Hear what is missing. Two-thirds of this page is "unknown": for an instrument whose very form is unsettled, its sound should be the last thing discussed.
