Yue

One character, four identities — instrument, measure, bellows, key

1 min read183 characters条目Chinese WindsUpdated 2026-09-27

The dictionary lists an instrument first and a measure second: bamboo pipes for music, then the measures yue, ge, sheng, dou, hu. One tube, blown at one end and filled at the other.

Four uses of one hollow tube

As a measure, the yue is the smallest unit: two make a ge, ten ge a sheng. Laozi asks whether the space between heaven and earth is not like a bellows. The Book of Documents has a key turning in a lock.

Four things share one character, and the common property is hollowness: not branches grown from an instrument, but one empty tube used differently. Blown, it sounds; filled, it counts; fitted to a bellows, it drives air; pushed into a lock, it opens.

The character is older than the texts

The oracle-bone form draws several tubes side by side, open at the ends. Guo Moruo read bound pipes, not bound slips, and said a later dictionary read ethics into a picture and lost the point.

Han commentators say three holes, the Mao commentary six, a later glossary seven. Modern scholars, starting from the character, return to bound pipes and call it a possible ancestor of the paixiao. Three readings, no object, no verdict.

One detail may reconcile them: old texts describe a short pipe for blowing and a long one for dancing. The contradictory counts may never have belonged to one object.

A range that is a placeholder

No object, no measurement. The figure gives A4 to D5, guessed from the three-hole reading.

Drawing what is unknown beats a handsome fake range. If the three-hole reading holds, it never had many notes.

The pipe that measured a country

Han officials filled the yellow-bell pitch pipe with millet: twelve hundred grains, one yue. The unit of capacity grows from there into ge, sheng, dou and hu. Li Shangyin asks why granary measures are bronze, and answers that bronze keeps its volume through heat and frost.

In Zhou ritual it was also a dance prop: pipe in the left hand, pheasant plume in the right, the civil dance, while the martial dance carries shields and axes. A master of the yue taught it, and the annals record the pipe withdrawn when the martial dance entered.

The character travels from instrument to granary to keyhole because it is hollow, not because it is poetic.

Sources

  • 字形（最早的"档案"）：「在甲骨文中，『籥』本作『龠』，象编管之形，似为排箫之前身」；郭沫若《甲骨文字研究》：「（龠）象形。象形者，象编管之形也。金文之作若者实示管头之空，示此为编管而非编简……许书反以侖理释之，大悖古意」；《说文》：「龠，乐之竹管，三孔，以和众声也。从品 · 侖」依古籍与文字学研究资料
  • 形制三说（无定论）：「籥」的形态有三说：一说为三孔单管（汉代郑玄注《周礼》《礼记》「籥，如篴，三孔」）；二说为六孔管乐器（《诗经 · 国风 · 简兮》汉代郑玄笺「籥，六孔」，毛传亦「籥，六孔」）；三说为编管吹奏乐器（杨荫浏《中国古代音乐史稿》「籥，卜辞中作……象编管吹奏乐器之形，可能是后来排箫的前身」）；「虽然当代学者对于『籥』的器型和发展多有争论，但是依然缺少明确的出土文物的证明，故也无定论」；《博雅》另记「龠谓之笛，有七孔」；「有吹籥 · 舞籥两种，吹籥短，三孔，舞籥长，六孔，可执之作舞具」；《尔雅 · 释乐》「大籥谓之产，其中谓之仲，小者谓之箹」依古籍与音乐史资料
  • 四个身份（字义）：① 乐器（《说文》「乐之竹管」；《周礼 · 春官 · 笙师》「掌教龡竽 · 笙 · 埙 · 籥 · 箫 · 篪 · 笛 · 管」）② 量器 · 容量单位（《汉书 · 律历志》「量者，龠 · 合 · 升 · 斗 · 斛也，所以量多少也，本起于黄钟之龠……二龠为合，十合为升，十升为斗，十斗为斛」；「龠者，黄钟律之实……容千二百黍」；《广雅 · 释器》「龠二曰合」）③ 鼓风管（橐龠）（《老子》「天地之间，其犹橐龠乎」）④ 钥匙（《尚书 · 金縢》「启籥见书」；《说文》「籥，书僮竹笘也」）依古籍与辞书资料
