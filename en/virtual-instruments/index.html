Virtual instruments and plug-ins

RAM killed the hardware, openness won the ecosystem — sample libraries and the four sockets

⭐⭐ The main line: RAM killed the hardware, openness won the ecosystem

「The hardware samplers' collapse was not about sound quality — they died at the ceiling of physical RAM: when EastWest released that orchestral library needing 32GB of disk space in 2003, all the hardware went dumb — most hardware samplers of the era could hold only 128MB」.

⭐⭐ A revolution's cause is often boring: RAM. The hardware samplers weren't beaten — they couldn't fit — 128MB of RAM met a 32GB orchestra.

⭐⭐ And after Kontakt seized the opening, it made the choice this site knows best: 「Native Instruments did not lock the sampler into a closed product but handed out the development tools — the KSP scripting language lets third-party developers build extraordinarily complex instrument logic」. ⭐⭐ 「What NI sells is not a sampler but a sampler development platform.」 (Against Moog's open volt-control standard versus Buchla's closed system — the same choice, won a different way.)

⭐⭐ Network effects: compatibility is the widest moat

⭐⭐ 「Composers accumulated hundreds of Kontakt libraries, and after upgrading to Kontakt 8 they could still load sound colours bought more than a decade earlier」 — against MIDI 1.0 staying viable for nearly four decades: two platforms that 「outlived their companies」 win the same thing: never dropping their users.

⭐⭐ The other face of the wall: the free Kontakt Player loads only pre-authorised libraries; the full version alone can import and edit your own samples — ⭐⭐ the openness faces developers; for users it is always tiered — the moat is also a wall. (Against eurorack's module democracy: that river has no gatekeeper.)

⭐⭐ Against the midi-controller entry's 1983 NAMM: MIDI was 「rivals building one standard together」; plug-in formats are 「everyone builds their own socket, plus adapters」 — the USB-versus-Lightning fight for sound arrived in the software world forty years later.

⭐⭐ Democratisation: one bedroom and one concert hall

⭐⭐ The technical trio: 「multi-sampling (each note at multiple velocity layers) · velocity layers (timbre change with loudness) · round-robin (multiple takes of each note — reducing the machine-gun effect)」 plus Kontakt scripting's 「intelligent legato transitions, true dynamic crossfading, adaptive articulation switching」.

⭐⭐ 「A $200 laptop and free plugins could score an indie film」 — against the DX7's preset distribution: virtual instruments turned the 「whole orchestra」 into something downloadable — between one bedroom producer and Hans Zimmer there was once an entire concert hall.

⭐ The AI entrance

⭐⭐ Kontakt 7 (2023) introduced AI-assisted sample cleanup — from the Fairlight's light pen drawing waveforms to AI cleaning samples: the tools for 「making sound」 are themselves being automated (against Schaeffer's musique concrète — the automation of editing began half a century ago).
