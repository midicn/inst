Pengling (bells)

A pair of tiny goblet-shaped bells whose old name is "star"

A pair of goblet bells

The mouth flares at the top; the body narrows to a hemispherical bowl about three centimetres across, the fixing point and the hole for the cord. No clapper: it is not shaken, it is collided.

Two make a set, one in each hand, rims meeting — the thinnest part. It belongs with the cymbals: a large thin disc, a small deep cup.

Bell-shaped, and unpitched

A closed shell locks a pitch most easily, as the bianzhong proves. But when two bells collide, where they meet and how they couple differ every time, and the pitch will not hold. Five sources agree: no definite pitch. The shape offered a pitch; the way it sounds withholds it.

A dozen names

Star was the old name, still used in the Qing; from the Tang and Song it was the bell cymbal, as a Qing court manual still has it. Regionally: touch-bell, twin star, clash bell; in Shaanxi the shaker; in Tibetan dingxia, in Naxi dingxiang.

Sheng-sheng and shui-shui go another route: the sound itself becomes the name. That star and bell cymbal coexist records a difficulty — the shape lies between the two, and documents call it bronze cymbal one moment, bell cymbal the next.

One hand is enough

In an ensemble it is usually played by someone who doubles. Peking opera gives it an exact post: behind a dan's fan-erhuang aria it marks the beat inside the singing and ornaments with the drum in the interludes.

Tibetan Toe and Nangma songs have women play it as they sing; temples use it for chanting. It often pairs with the small wooden fish — one wood, one metal, two points.

It can also be one bell on a stick, struck with a metal slip. Resonant bronze rings long, brass short: on something this small, the material is the whole tonal design.

  • 「外观呈小钟形，可算是钹的变形；用硬模铸造而成，有半球形和圆锥形两种；通常铃高 4.5–6 厘米 · 铃口直径 5.5–7 厘米，中空无舌，铃顶隆起半球形作为固定点，称作碗或帽，碗径 3 厘米」；「藏族丁夏规格较大，铃高 6–8 厘米 · 铃口直径 7–8 厘米」依通行乐器资料
