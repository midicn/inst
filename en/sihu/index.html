Sihu

Four strings, two pitch groups — one bow sounds both at once

Related

ErhuBanhuYehuZhuihuRegisterTimbre

This page was supposed to open with a contradiction: the instrument is called "four-string huqin", yet some say it really has only two strings.

What the sources actually say is more interesting than a contradiction: the name is truthful — it really does have four strings. But the next layer is better: four strings, two pitches.

「四胡，因张 4 根弦故名」; 「1、3 弦为同度音，2、4 弦为同度音」

⭐⭐ Main line 56: one bow, two voices — "four" counts the strings, not the notes.

⭐⭐ How one bow sounds two pitches

This is the core of the page, and the part most often misunderstood.

It is not "four strings played separately". It does this:

「鬃弓分作两股夹入其间，同时拉响两组弦。」「弓毛的两股马尾分别穿过一、二弦和三、四弦的中间，弓子向外靠就是拉外弦，向内靠就是拉内弦。」

Picture it: the bow hair is split into two strands, one passing between strings 1 and 2, one between strings 3 and 4. Pull it and both groups speak.

⭐ That also explains its other alias, 「二夹弦」 — ⭐⭐ one bow clamping two string groups.

⭐⭐ Why three tunings? Because the tuning gives way to the voice

The four strings split into an outer pair (1·3) and an inner pair (2·4), a perfect fifth apart. But different places tune it differently:

NameOuterInnerUsed for
Mongolian high-pitched sihua¹d²Mongolian music, higher
Er ren tai sihu (Inner Mongolia)g¹c²accompanying er ren tai
Mongolian mid-range sihu#f¹b¹old artists' own singing in "silk-string seated singing"

⭐⭐ Why? The research literature is unusually direct:

they 「在定弦 · 演奏时主要考虑嗓音的高度…… 乐器演奏都是为了演唱的便捷而服务。」

  • Er ren tai: tuned g¹–c² because, in the interview,「考虑演员好唱，嗓子不费劲」;
  • later changed because a local folk arts troupe wanted「提高音量 · 音高」;
  • the mid-range sihu sits lower because those singers'「嗓音都不是很高，为了好唱」.

⭐⭐ This is the most important line on the page. A tuning is not a property of the instrument — it is a property of the human voice. This is the same thread as the zhuihu "staying close to the singing voice" and the yehu "dropping an octave to take the supporting part": across this whole bowed-string section, the pitch of these instruments is repeatedly decided by a sung line.

⭐⭐ "Pinging" and "stopping": two hands, one instrument

Besides the stopping technique both Mongolian and Han players use, older Mongolian artists use a special "top-string" technique — and this has fed back into the instrument's shape:

the differences in the instrument's form「源于这一技法差异」— 「蒙古四胡的琴弦离琴杆的距离远，而二人台四胡则不需要」, and that distance「影响了琴轴的变化以及琴码的位置」.

⭐⭐ The same pattern again: how the hand works decides how the instrument is made. (The yehu's coconut shell gained a foot because it was unstable; here, the fingering decides the distance from string to neck.)

⭐⭐ Three names, three languages

⭐⭐ The three names point to three different worlds: 「四胡」 counts strings, 「胡尔·朝勒」 is Mongolian, 「提琴」 is the Qing court's scholarly renaming. An instrument with three names belongs to two linguistic traditions at once — that says more about it than the string count.

Form: "slightly longer and thicker" than the erhu

「形制与二胡相同，琴杆用红木或乌木制，略长而粗，置四轴，琴头平顶无饰，琴筒为木制，有圆筒形 · 六角形 · 八角形之分，一端蒙以羊皮或蟒皮。」

⭐⭐ "Slightly longer and thicker" is the fingerprint of the four-string requirement: two extra groups need a thicker neck for four pegs, and a longer neck and body for two unison pairs to resonate. The same logic as the gehu, which lay horizontal to supply the bass — form is forced by what the instrument has to do.

⚠️ One honest uncertainty on this page: the bow length differs across sources — one gives 「琴弓长 75 厘米」 with the hair split in two, another gives 60 cm. ⭐ This page deliberately does not pick one figure: both may describe different forms or different measuring conventions.

⭐⭐ An accompaniment list spanning half the country

Narrative singing: Jingyun dagu, Xihe dagu, Tianjin shidiao, Hubei xiaoqu, Shaoxing lianhualuo. Opera and puppetry: er ren tai (with dizi and yangqin, plus clappers and wooden blocks for rhythm), quju, pishadow.

Where: 「流行于蒙古国东部与中国内蒙古 · 东北和华北各省、区」.

⭐⭐ Why can a grassland bowed instrument accompany genres from Beijing to Shaoxing to Hubei? ⭐ Because it is a walking instrument — travelling singers take it wherever they sing.

Heritage: er ren tai, first national list (2006)

Er ren tai is named for the fact that all of its content is「两人对歌对舞」, and it is

accompanied by dizi, sihu and yangqin; er ren tai was listed in 2006 in China's first batch of national intangible cultural heritage.

⭐⭐ That completes the name story: the instrument belongs not only to the Mongolian tradition but also lives inside er ren tai and Jingyun dagu — a name that carries a people and a travelling singing style at once.

  • ⭐⭐ 定弦（三种，与嗓音有关——本页最实用的一段）：四胡分四根弦为外弦（1·3 同度）与内弦（2·4 同度），里外音程关系为纯五度；蒙古高音四胡定弦外弦 a¹ · 内弦 d²；二人台四胡定弦外弦 g¹ · 内弦 c²；蒙古中音四胡（老艺人"丝弦坐唱"用）外弦 #f¹ · 内弦 b¹；⭐ 「定弦 … 主要考虑嗓音的高度 … 都是为了演唱的便捷而服务」
  • ⭐⭐ "顶弦"与"按弦"两种技法：蒙汉艺人在"按弦"之外，蒙古族老艺人会使用一种「顶弦」的特殊技法；⚠️ 形制差异（蒙古四胡琴弦离琴杆距离远，二人台四胡不需要）源于这一技法差异（影响琴轴变化与琴码位置）
  • ⭐⭐ 地域分布（一件"走路"的乐器）：四胡「流行于蒙古国东部与中国内蒙古 · 东北和华北各省、区」；⚠️ 属地按来源原样记录
  • ⭐⭐ 伴奏对象（跨度极大）：为京韵大鼓 · 西河大鼓 · 天津时调 · 湖北小曲 · 绍兴莲花落等说唱，以及二人台 · 曲剧 · 皮影等戏曲伴奏
  • ⭐⭐ 蒙古族与汉族共同乐器：四胡「源于古代奚琴，乃蒙古族和汉族的共同乐器」；亦为「二人台」的主要伴奏乐器之一（与笛子 · 扬琴并列，另辅以梆子与四块瓦）
  • ⭐ 代表曲目：《赶路》 · 《八音》 · 《莫德列马》
  • ⭐ 二人台与长调（非遗背景）：二人台「因演出内容皆以两人对歌对舞而命名」，主要伴奏乐器包括笛子 · 四胡 · 扬琴，另辅以梆子和四块瓦等敲击乐器以加强节奏感；2006 年被列入中国第一批《国家级非物质文化遗产名录》依中国香港康乐及文化事务署「民族音乐巡迴展览·内蒙古篇」资料
