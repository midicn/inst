Paigu (set of tuned drums)

Five drums, ten notes — and every one of them can be retuned on the spot

1 min read65 characters条目Chinese PercussionUpdated 2026-09-26

Five drums in a row, graded from large to small. Two heads each, ten notes — every one changeable on the spot. Most drums have the pitch they were built with.

Five drums, ten notes

The row is what the name means. The shells stand twenty-nine to thirty-three centimetres tall, the top heads sixteen point seven to thirty-seven across, on an adjustable stand topped with a U-shaped cradle.

Two notes from one drum because the hoops share an outer diameter and differ inside: same size outside, different free area, different pitch. Five times two is ten.

One turn in the cradle brings the other head to the hand: ten notes you can swap.

Keeping the pitch adjustable

Bells, chimes and gongs all lay instruments out as a scale. The paigu leaves the pitch adjustable instead.

Each head has a tuner with a range of a fourth or a fifth, set before playing. A gong's pitch is forged, a chime's ground; neither can change, and both are pure and stable. The paigu keeps pitch as a knob, and one set serves many pieces.

Wood and hide, no precision mechanism — yet two notes per drum, each tunable.

An instrument that was developed

In the early 1960s Cai Huiquan and Yang Jingming of the Central Broadcasting Chinese Orchestra built it on the tanggu, borrowing the way Zhejiang wind-and-percussion pairs a mid-sized tanggu with a waist drum. It was first called the set drum.

Then a thirteen-drum version by Yu Yanhe, fibreglass shells, plug-in mounting; a one-piece stand by Liu Hanlin that comes apart for transport.

It is now a core instrument in professional percussion teaching. Tradition is not a list finished in the past; it is a list still taking names.

Tune first, then run

The pitches run along a line, so running becomes travelling. The sticks want points, not a boom.

Tuning comes first, the step that separates it from every other drum here: others are tuned instruments, this one a tuned scale. Afterwards it leads rather than accompanies, with solos in Festival in the Mountain Village.

The sources say the inner diameters differ and so do the pitches, never which way. What is certain is that the inner diameter sets the pitch, not the outward size.

Paigu has no definite pitch; tuning is relative, by head tension.

Sources

  • 「排鼓是鼓类乐器的新品种，出现于 20 世纪 60 年代初，由中央广播民族乐团蔡惠泉 · 杨竞明等根据民间常用的中型堂鼓和腰鼓改革制作而成，以后又不断有所改进」依通行乐器资料
  • 「另有资料说明它是根据浙江吹打乐中中型堂鼓和腰鼓的组合特点、在堂鼓基础上研制而成，称作「套鼓」，也叫排鼓」依通行乐器资料
  • 「排鼓由五个大小不同 · 发音有别的鼓组成一套；鼓身高 29 至 33.3 厘米，上口面直径 16.7 至 37 厘米，下口面直径 11.7 至 37 厘米」（另有资料记作「一般由四至七只组成」）依通行乐器资料
  • 「每个鼓的两面鼓框外径相同而内径不一，可发出音高不同的两个音来，故五个鼓有十个音」依通行乐器资料；⚠️ 来源只说明「内径不一 → 音高不同」，未说明方向，本页因此不补方向（见「常见误解」）
  • 「每个鼓的两面都装有调音设备，调音幅度可达四或五度，可根据实际需要定音」依通行乐器资料
  • 「鼓身固定在一个可以升降的特制鼓架上，鼓架上端为「U」形架，鼓身置于架中可以翻转进行音高选择」依通行乐器资料
  • 「排鼓也是定音鼓，其两面都装有调音设备」依通行乐器资料
  • 「排鼓发音激烈 · 跳荡；高音坚实有力，中 · 低音宽厚洪亮，尤擅于表现热烈欢腾的情绪」依通行乐器资料
  • 「在乐曲《山寨的节日》《海上锣鼓》《龙船锣鼓》中，均有排鼓的独奏乐段」；独奏曲目另包括《鼓上飞舞》《赛龙舟》《散敲》等依通行乐器资料
  • 「吉林省歌舞剧院于延河与苏州民族乐器三厂合作，研制成由十三面鼓组成的排鼓，鼓身用玻璃钢制作，鼓和架为插口式结合，已用于舞台演出」依通行乐器资料
  • 「整体架五音排鼓由刘汉林与济南鼓韵打击乐器厂王令新共同设计制作，在原来分体架的基础上组合成整体架，演出前方便移动、适合赶场，可以拆装，也方便长途运输」依通行乐器资料
  • 「排鼓用于民乐合奏 · 音乐会表演及专业教学，并被纳入中国打击乐专业教学体系作为核心乐器」依通行乐器资料
  • ⚠️ 同名异物：「曹屯排鼓 · 庆源排鼓（相传起源于北宋）· 长信排鼓等被列入省级非物质文化遗产名录」——这些指的是各地的民间鼓乐，与上面这件 20 世纪 60 年代研制的民族乐队乐器不是同一件事
  • 「排鼓属于八音中的革」依通行资料
